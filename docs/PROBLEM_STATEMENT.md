# The problem we solve — and how to say it

Isaac's direction, 2026-09-04, after seeing the thirty-second film. This
is the reference for the film, the website and the store listing. Where an
earlier document says something different (`docs/FILM_BRIEF.md` §3,
`docs/POSITIONING_REVIEW.md` §"The message"), this one wins.

## The problem, in the customer's words

You hear it on a podcast. Morning light. Protein first. A walk after
dinner. Lights down by nine. You think: that's brilliant. Then Tuesday
happens, and it's gone.

Nobody researches it, writes it down and puts it into their week. So it
never happens.

That is the problem. It is not "too many apps that don't talk to each
other" — nobody has all those apps, and the framing was ours, not theirs.
The real gap is between hearing good advice and living it. Podcasts are
popular because the advice is good and free; almost none of it survives
contact with a normal week, because the work of turning it into a plan is
the work nobody does.

**IntentNorth does that part.** It takes what you would do if someone did
the research for you, grades it, and places it into your actual week —
then moves it when your week moves and tells you why.

## Say it this way

- **Lead with the moment everyone recognises**: hearing the advice,
  thinking it's brilliant, losing it by Tuesday. It is true of every
  market we serve and needs no product knowledge to land.
- **Then the gap**: nobody researches it, writes it down and puts it into
  their week.
- **Then the product, in one sentence**: one iPhone app that turns what
  you hear into a plan for your actual week.
- **Then the proof**, one idea per beat: graded and sourced practices, one
  tap into the week, a program for each part of life, the day already
  decided with its reason, a short night changing the session, levels
  earned from what you log.
- **Close on the line**: *What you'd do if someone did the research for
  you.*

## Do not say

- **The number of practices.** "177 practices" does not sound impressive
  to a customer and invites the wrong comparison. Say what the practices
  are instead: graded A to E for how strong the evidence is, with the
  sources named and where each one stops. The efficacy and the research
  grading are the point; the count is not.
- **"Protocols."** People hear hospitals. Practices, or simply what the
  expert said.
- **"Life operating system", "operating plan", "the ladder", "pathways",
  "arbitration".** See the glossary in `docs/POSITIONING_REVIEW.md`.
- **A problem the customer does not have.** No four-apps opener, no
  "dashboard" language, no "optimise".
