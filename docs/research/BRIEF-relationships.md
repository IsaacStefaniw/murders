# Brief: the Relationship and Family coaches

Read `docs/research/README.md` first. Two coaches, one pillar — write both
in this round because the literature overlaps heavily.

## Current state

Connection: 31 protocols — **A 0** · B 2 · C 14 · D 14 · E 1.

**An integrity test forbids an A grade in this pillar.** That is
deliberate and it stays. Nothing about couple or family behaviour has
support at the level of morning light or strength training, and the
library says so rather than pretending otherwise. If you believe you have
found an A, write it up in `findings.md` with the evidence and let a human
decide — do not attempt to ship it.

This is the pillar where the gap between popular confidence and actual
evidence is widest in the entire project. Treat every famous finding as
guilty until checked.

## The replication problem, named

Relationship science has had a hard decade. Several of its most
recognisable results have shrunk, failed to replicate, or turned out to
rest on methods that would not pass review today. Specific things to check
rather than assume:

- **Gottman's ratio and the prediction accuracy claims.** The predictive
  work has been criticised substantively for how it was validated. Much of
  the underlying observational work is real and useful; the popularised
  precision is not. Grade what the papers support.
- **Attachment style as a stable trait** applied to adult couples — the
  consumer version runs far ahead of the research.
- **The five love languages**, which has been tested and has not held up.
- **Birth order effects on personality**, largely not supported in large
  samples.
- **Most of what is claimed about "date night"** as a causal intervention.

Naming these is as valuable as adding practices. The library already names
the five stages of grief as unsupported and excludes psychological
debriefing entirely — one of the few interventions whose evidence points
the wrong way. Keep that standard.

## Where the depth is missing — relationship

- **Conflict repair.** What the observational work actually supports about
  recovery after an argument, as distinct from avoiding one.
- **Responsiveness** — Harry Reis and Shelly Gable's work on how a partner
  responds to good news is better evidenced than most of this field.
- **Division of labour**, including the mental load, which has real
  measurement behind it now.
- **Money and couples.** Joint versus separate accounts has actual
  research. Cross-reference the money brief.
- **Long distance, and shift-work couples** — where one partner's roster
  makes shared time structurally hard. Under-covered everywhere and
  relevant to this audience.
- **Relationship transitions**: new baby, empty nest, retirement,
  separation. All likely `neverNag`.

## Where the depth is missing — family

- **Co-parenting**, including after separation.
- **Teenagers.** The library is thin here and it is where parents actually
  struggle. Autonomy support, monitoring versus surveillance, and what the
  evidence says about screens — which is far more equivocal than the
  headlines.
- **Ageing parents and the carer's own hours.** Already partly covered,
  and worth going deeper: carer burden is well studied and the app has a
  coach that can act on it.
- **Sibling relationships in adulthood.**
- **Family routines and rituals**, which is one of the better-evidenced
  areas in family research.
- **Blended families.**

## Friendship — the quiet gap

Adult friendship is under-served across the whole category and there is
more research than people expect: how friendships are maintained, what
frequency of contact sustains them, and the loneliness literature, which
is large and has genuine health outcomes attached.

The social connection and mortality work is among the strongest evidence
in this pillar. It may still not be an A under this library's rules — it
is observational — but it deserves a careful B or C and clear reasoning.

## Sources worth starting from

**Journals:** Journal of Marriage and Family · Journal of Social and
Personal Relationships · Personal Relationships · Developmental Psychology
· Journal of Family Psychology · Child Development · Perspectives on
Psychological Science (for the replication commentary)

**Researchers:** Harry Reis and Shelly Gable (responsiveness, capitalisation)
· Eli Finkel (marriage, and unusually honest about limits) · Julianne
Holt-Lunstad (social connection and mortality) · Andrew Fuligni (family
relationships) · Amie Gordon

**Podcasts, as discovery only:** treat this area's podcasts with more
suspicion than any other. The genre is dominated by practitioners selling
courses, and the confident, mechanistic framing they use is almost never
supported at the level implied. Use them to find topics; never to grade.

## Safety and tone

Several of these practices sit near real distress. Anything touching
separation, bereavement, family violence or a child's wellbeing needs a
safety note that routes to a professional, and nothing in this pillar
should imply that a behavioural practice substitutes for help.

Family violence in particular: if a practice could be read as advice to
stay and work on a relationship where someone is unsafe, do not write it.

`neverNag` liberally here. Someone three weeks after a separation does not
need an adherence score.

## What a good round looks like

15-20 new protocols weighted toward conflict repair, teenagers, carers and
adult friendship. Honest C and D grades throughout, no A. Five or six
contradicted practices, which this pillar has more of than any other. And
an explicit list of famous findings you checked and declined to use.
