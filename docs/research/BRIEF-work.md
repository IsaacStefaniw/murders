# Brief: the Work & Leadership coach

Read `docs/research/README.md` first.

## Current state

23 protocols — A 2 · B 4 · C 5 · D 9 · **E 3**. The weakest-graded pillar
in the library, and that is honest rather than a failure: most management
advice is untested, and D means "what experienced people do, ahead of the
research".

Your job is partly to find the parts that *are* tested, and partly to keep
the rest graded truthfully while the temptation to inflate is strongest.
Business content is where confident assertion is most rewarded and least
earned.

## A decision already made

Isaac decided that **hands-on work gets the shutdown ritual and the weekly
shape, not a focus block**. A tradesperson, a nurse or a chef does not have
a two-hour deep-work window and offering one is the app telling them it
does not understand their job. That decision stands and shapes this round:
roughly half the audience is not sitting at a desk.

Most work-and-productivity research studies knowledge workers. Say so when
you use it, and look actively for the literature on shift work, physical
work and frontline roles — it exists, it is smaller, and it is far more
relevant to Isaac's association and employer targets.

## Where the depth is missing

**Recovery from work.** Sabine Sonnentag's body of work on psychological
detachment is one of the better-evidenced things in this whole pillar, and
it connects directly to the shutdown ritual the app already runs. Go deep.

**Burnout, properly.** Maslach's construct, what the evidence says about
individual versus organisational intervention, and the uncomfortable
finding that most individual-level burnout interventions do little when
the job itself is the cause. The app should not imply a person can breathe
their way out of an unsustainable role.

**Shift work.** Rosters, the drive home, sleep and safety, and what
actually helps. Overlaps the recovery brief — coordinate rather than
duplicate, and cross-reference protocol ids.

**Meetings.** There is real research here and it is unusually actionable.

**The progress principle.** Amabile's diary work on small wins is a
genuine finding with a real mechanism, and it maps onto what the app
already does with milestones.

**Feedback and performance conversations.** A large literature, and a
surprising amount of it says the popular practices do not work.

**Autonomy, and job crafting.** Better evidenced than most of what gets
sold as engagement.

**Return-to-work and career transitions.** Redundancy is a life
transition; anything you write here likely wants `neverNag`.

## Time back — what people can stop

Framed as a gift, not a lecture. Someone who stops doing a thing that does
not work gets those minutes back for something that does. Keep this to
roughly a fifth of the round.

The library already names the "23 minutes to refocus" figure and the claim
that micro-breaks raise output. Likely more:

- Multitasking training and the idea it improves with practice
- Most personality instruments used for team composition, MBTI first among
  them
- The feedback sandwich
- Open-plan offices as collaboration drivers, which has actual measurement
  against it
- Learning styles applied to workplace training — already named in the
  learning context, worth naming here too
- Most of what is claimed for brainstorming in groups versus alone
- Growth mindset interventions as delivered at scale, where the effect
  sizes have come down considerably under replication

## Sources worth starting from

**Journals:** Journal of Applied Psychology · Journal of Occupational
Health Psychology · Academy of Management Journal · Organizational
Behavior and Human Decision Processes · Work & Stress · Human Relations ·
Occupational and Environmental Medicine (for shift work)

**Researchers:** Sabine Sonnentag (recovery and detachment) · Christina
Maslach (burnout) · Teresa Amabile (progress principle) · Adam Grant ·
Amy Edmondson (psychological safety — check how the evidence has held up
as it became fashionable) · Gloria Mark (attention and interruption)

**Podcasts, as discovery only:** *WorkLife* (Adam Grant) · *Deep
Questions* (Cal Newport). Business podcasts are the weakest discovery
source of any area in this project — the genre runs on confident
anecdote. Use them to find names, never claims.

## Scheduling shape

The shutdown ritual is `anchor.deadline`-shaped for some people and
`sleep`-anchored for others. Deep work blocks want `duringWork: true`.
Anything you write for shift workers must survive a work window that
crosses midnight — the scheduler handles it now, but a protocol that
assumes a 9-to-5 will place badly.

## What a good round looks like

15-20 new protocols weighted toward recovery from work, burnout, shift
work and non-desk roles. The grade spread should stay honest — if it comes
back with far more B than the current pillar has, check your reasoning —
though the recovery-from-work literature genuinely is stronger than the
rest of this pillar and should be graded that way.
Five or six contradicted practices, which this area has in abundance.

## What this gives the Work coach to say

Half this audience is not at a desk. The coach should be able to say:

- "You're off at six. Do the two-minute close-down and then genuinely stop
  — the detachment is the part that does the work."
- "Four nights on this week. Here's the shape that protects the drive
  home."
- "That review went badly. Here's the thing worth doing tomorrow, and it
  isn't working later."
- "You finished the big one. Take the win before the next thing starts."

Be honest about limits too: where a job itself is the cause of burnout, the
coach should say so kindly rather than implying a breathing practice will
fix an unsustainable role. Naming that is respectful, not defeatist.
