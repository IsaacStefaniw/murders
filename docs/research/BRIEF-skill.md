# Brief: the Skill & Craft pillar

Read `docs/research/README.md` first.

## Current state

22 protocols — A 2 · B 9 · C 7 · D 3 · E 1. **The best-evidenced pillar in
the library relative to its size**, because learning science is one of the
few areas here with genuinely strong replicated findings. Retrieval
practice and spaced review are honestly A, and a previous round graded
them *up* on that basis.

This is the lowest-priority brief of the nine. Run it last, or run it
short. The value is in a handful of additions and a check that nothing has
drifted.

## Where the depth is missing

- **Interleaving**, as distinct from spacing. Well tested, frequently
  confused with variety for its own sake.
- **Feedback timing** in motor learning — the literature is more
  counterintuitive than people expect, and immediate feedback is not
  always better.
- **Mental practice and imagery**, which has real evidence in motor
  domains.
- **Sleep and consolidation**, cross-referenced with the recovery brief.
- **Plateaus**, and what the evidence says about breaking them, as opposed
  to what coaches say.
- **Transfer** — the honest and rather deflating answer about how little
  skill transfers between domains. Worth writing precisely because it
  contradicts so much of what is sold.
- **Learning as an adult**, including instrument, language and trade
  skills, where most of the popular content assumes a school-age learner.

## Already named, and worth extending

`LEARNING_ANTIPATTERNS` is first-class content in this library and already
carries highlighting, rereading and learning styles. The library also
names the ten-thousand-hours framing, blocked repetition and internal
body-part cueing.

Extend it. Candidates worth checking:

- The 21-day habit formation figure, which traces to a plastic surgeon's
  observation, not a study — the actual research on habit formation timing
  gives a very different and much wider answer
- Massed practice and cramming, which works for a test and not for
  retention
- "Practice makes perfect" as against practice-makes-permanent
- Note-taking by hand versus laptop, where the famous result has had a
  troubled replication history
- Most claims about critical periods applied to adult learners

## Sources worth starting from

**Journals:** Psychological Science · Journal of Experimental Psychology:
Learning, Memory and Cognition · Educational Psychology Review ·
Psychological Bulletin · Journal of Motor Behavior · Human Movement
Science

**Researchers:** Robert and Elizabeth Bjork (desirable difficulties) ·
Henry Roediger and Jeffrey Karpicke (retrieval practice) · Mark Guadagnoli
and Timothy Lee (challenge point) · Gabriele Wulf (external focus of
attention) · Anders Ericsson's original work, read directly rather than
through its popularisation

**Podcasts, as discovery only:** the learning-science community publishes
well and openly; prefer their papers and blogs to podcast coverage, which
tends to compress the findings into slogans.

## What a good round looks like

8-12 new protocols. Several antipattern additions, which may be the more
valuable half. And a verification pass on the two A grades — they are
almost certainly right, but a library that grades honestly should be able
to show its working on its strongest claims.
