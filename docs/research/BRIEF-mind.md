# Brief: the Mind pillar

Read `docs/research/README.md` first.

## Current state

40 protocols — A 1 · B 15 · C 18 · D 5 · E 1. The **largest pillar in the
library**, and reasonably well graded. This is a depth-and-honesty round,
not an expansion round. If you come back with fifteen thin additions you
have made the library worse.

Mind also drives real in-app sessions: guided meditation with voice,
breathing, journalling. Anything with `sessionType: 'breathe'` runs
through a player a person actually sits through, so the duration and the
shape have to be right.

## The gap that matters most: adverse effects

**Meditation has adverse effects, they are documented, and almost nobody
in the consumer category will say so.** Willoughby Britton's work at Brown
is the reference point, and the prevalence figures are higher than most
people assume — meaningful proportions of practitioners report distressing
experiences, and for some the effects are lasting.

The library should carry this honestly. Not as a warning that frightens
people off a practice with real benefits, but as the safety note that
every other app omits. This is a differentiator of exactly the kind
IntentNorth is built on, and it is the single most valuable thing you
could add to this pillar.

It matters commercially too: the app puts a guided voice in someone's ear
for ten minutes. It should know what it is doing.

## Where the depth is missing

- **Rumination**, as distinct from worry, and what actually interrupts it.
- **Self-compassion versus self-esteem.** Kristin Neff's work — check how
  it has held up, some of it is stronger than the surrounding field.
- **Grief.** The library already names the five stages as unsupported.
  What does the evidence support? Continuing bonds, and the distinction
  between grief and depression.
- **Behavioural activation.** One of the better-evidenced things in the
  whole of clinical psychology and unusually well suited to an app that
  schedules a day. It should probably be prominent here.
- **Worry postponement and scheduled worry time** — small literature,
  genuinely tested.
- **Expressive writing.** Pennebaker's paradigm has been studied for
  decades; effect sizes are modest and the honest grade is probably lower
  than its fame suggests.
- **Nature exposure.** Real effects, heavily overclaimed in the popular
  version. The "20 minutes" figures are far more specific than the
  evidence.
- **Loneliness**, cross-referenced with the relationships brief.

## Where to be careful

This pillar sits closest to clinical territory of any in the library.

- **Never write treatment.** CBT and ACT have strong evidence bases and
  the library may point at them by name and route to a professional. It
  may not deliver them.
- Anything touching depression, anxiety disorders, PTSD or self-harm needs
  a safety note that routes to real help, and Australian services should
  be nameable in copy where it fits.
- **Breathwork.** Some popularised protocols involve hyperventilation and
  breath holds and have real contraindications, including in water. Write
  the safety note properly or do not write the protocol.
- **Psychedelics are out of scope.** Whatever the trial evidence now
  shows, this library does not write compounds, and this one especially.

## Contradicted practices to hunt

- Catharsis and venting as anger management, which has evidence against it
- Positive affirmations for people with low self-esteem, where the
  research suggests they can backfire
- Most brain-training claims about transfer to general cognition
- The popular version of the "power pose" literature
- Ego depletion as originally stated, which has largely not replicated
- Subliminal and sleep-learning claims

## Sources worth starting from

**Journals:** Clinical Psychology Review · JAMA Psychiatry · Behaviour
Research and Therapy · Mindfulness · Psychological Bulletin ·
Perspectives on Psychological Science · Journal of Consulting and Clinical
Psychology

**Researchers:** Willoughby Britton (adverse effects) · Judson Brewer ·
Richard Davidson · Kristin Neff (self-compassion) · James Pennebaker
(expressive writing) · Sona Dimidjian (behavioural activation)

**Podcasts, as discovery only:** be especially careful here. Meditation
and mental-health podcasts are heavily populated by teachers and
practitioners rather than researchers, and the claims made about
neuroscience in this space are frequently unsupported by the imaging work
they invoke.

## What a good round looks like

10-15 new protocols — fewer than other briefs, deliberately. The adverse
effects work, behavioural activation, rumination and grief are the
priorities. Several regrades, because a 40-protocol pillar written across
three earlier rounds will contain some drift. And a hard look at whether
the single A grade in this pillar deserves to be there.
