# Mind round — findings

The largest pillar in the library, and the brief was explicit that this is a depth and honesty round rather than an expansion one. Seven new cards, six regrades, and the safety work that is the actual point.

Output: `protocols.ts`, `sources.md`, `episodes.md`, `ladder.md`, and the Gate 1 and Gate 2 working in `ledgers/`.

## What was checked

48 papers opened at registry level, with the retraction check clean across all of them. Gate 1 mined 48 episodes for mind and skill together, which is the restructure working as intended. The literature inlet did most of the work here, for reasons in section 6.

## 1. The safety finding, which is the round

The pillar drives real guided sessions with voice. Its most-used meditation card is graded B and its entire safety line is seven words saying the practice is non-clinical with no therapeutic claims. A lower-use card graded C has a proper one naming distress, dissociation, and when to stop.

Verified this round from the primary literature:

| Finding | Status |
|---|---|
| About 1 in 10 people who have meditated report a functionally impairing adverse effect (434 of 953 screened) | **Verified** |
| Impairment lasting a month or more: about 1 in 80 | **Verified**, and the honest refinement to the headline |
| **60% of those reporting difficulties were meditation teachers** | **Verified verbatim** from the results section |

The teacher figure is the one that removes the comfortable explanation. This is not people practising wrongly.

**Two things follow.** A new card, `sitting-check`, whose content is the specific warning signs and whose function is to make stopping normal. And a product change that matters more than any copy change: **"you may stop at any time" belongs inside the session player**, not only on a card somebody read once before starting. A safety note reachable only outside the session is not reachable during it, which is when it is needed.

## 2. A correction to something Gate 1 reported, and that I relayed

Gate 1 reported a non-linear meditation dose curve with a threshold at about thirty minutes a day, taken from a transcript. **Verification could not find that threshold anywhere in the literature.**

What is real is smaller and still useful: a small randomised trial with polysomnography found dose-proportional increases in cortical arousal. The conclusion survives — the library's ten-minute dose is sound and is not obviously improved by lengthening — but the specific number does not exist, and the claim was carried one gate further than it should have been before anyone checked.

This is the two-gate structure doing its job, one gate late rather than after shipping.

## 3. What moved, and in both directions

**Three up.** Behavioural activation is now shown non-inferior to cognitive therapy across two independent meta-analyses seven years apart, while being far simpler; the brief said `one-small-act` should be the pillar's most prominent card and the evidence now says so too. The best-possible-self exercise has a dedicated meta-analysis it did not have when it was graded, and it endorses the shorter version. Gratitude writing survives a preregistered analysis across 24,804 people in 28 countries with publication-bias correction, which turns a small effect into a tested one.

**Three down, and one of them is the most-used card in the pillar.** `meditation-10` goes from tested to some-evidence: the brief-mindfulness meta-analysis covering 65 trials and 5,489 people finds g=0.21 with publication bias detected, and effects shrink further against active controls. Nothing about the practice changes. The copy should not get colder, only more specific, and its current appeal to what interviewed high performers do is an anecdote doing evidential work.

`evening-journal` loses a claim rather than a grade's worth of substance: its mood half holds and its **sleep half has no support**, with the nearest meta-analytic evidence null. `body-scan-sleep` goes to early days on six trials and 330 people with most sleep outcomes null, plus objectively measured arousal pointing the wrong way. It stays, because it is pleasant and harmless and expectancy is a real mechanism, but it should stop claiming a sleep-onset benefit.

**The pillar's only A survives a deliberate attempt to break it.** If-then planning holds on independent meta-analyses across clinical, motivational and substance-use domains, with 15,907 people in the best-powered one. The direction agrees everywhere. The copy should carry the modest effect size rather than the large one from the smallest study.

Net movement: 40% to 42.5% at A and B. Deliberately flat.

## 4. Time back

The phones claim resolved exactly as Gate 1 predicted when it flagged it as a false convergence. The widely quoted tally is 22 experiments with 16 positive, not 25 and 16, and the compilation is a self-maintained document rather than peer-reviewed. A 2025 preregistered meta-analysis of 10 studies covering 4,674 people is **null on every outcome and null on dose**. In adults, abstinence helped in 3 of 12 studies against 5 of 6 for therapy-based approaches.

So "reduce social media to feel better" grades early days. The library's attention and notification cards keep their B, because they rest on a different literature about divided attention at encoding. That distinction is the whole finding: the same behaviour, two different rationales, one supported and one not.

## 5. What we declined to build, and why it matters

The strongest evidence in this entire round is for mindfulness-based cognitive therapy preventing depressive relapse: an individual-patient meta-analysis across 1,258 patients with a hazard ratio of 0.69.

**The app must not deliver it.** It is treatment, it needs a clinician, and the honest thing is to name it, route to one, and say plainly that the best thing in this category is not what this app is. The same goes for rumination-focused therapy and for anything aimed at post-traumatic stress.

That is not a gap in the round. Knowing where the app stops is part of what makes the rest of it trustworthy.

## 6. Where the evidence came from, and why so many cards have no name on them

Six of seven cards ship with an empty attribution.

The mind and skill corpus returns **zero episodes** on behavioural activation, rumination, expressive writing, spaced repetition, deliberate practice, growth mindset, learning styles, breathwork or nature exposure. Eleven of the briefs' named researchers have no podcast presence between them.

This is the third pillar in a row to find it, and the pattern is now recorded in the pipeline: the more rigorous the underlying science, the less likely a podcast covers it. Podcast discovery finds what is being sold and argued about. The literature inlet finds what is true and quiet.

The single best podcast source in this round was a full free transcript in the Ferriss archive, with the researcher the brief named as its top priority. It was not on the aggregator at all.

## 7. What the Mind coach can now say on a Tuesday

- "How is the sitting actually going? More settled, or more wound up? Either answer is useful and you are allowed to stop."
- "Sleep first. If you are not sleeping, everything here works less well, and that is the thing to fix before we add anything."
- "Do the one small thing, even though you do not feel like it. Especially then. That one is as good as far more complicated treatments and it is the simplest thing in here."
- "Save it for the worry window. Write the line, put it in the slot, and come back to it at five."
- "Write about it three times this week and then stop. It is meant to end."
- "Two hours outside this week, however it lands. One long walk or six short ones, it made no difference in the research."
- "That is not a stage you are supposed to be at. There are no stages."

## 8. Product notes

- **"You may stop at any time" belongs in the session player.** Highest priority item in the round.
- **`open-monitoring`'s safety line is the best in the pillar and should be the template** for `meditation-10` and the other guided sessions.
- **`sitting-check` should probably surface after a run of sessions**, not only on its monthly schedule, because the people most at risk are the most engaged.
- **`two-hours-outside` and `outdoor-reset` should share a weekly counter** rather than existing as two separate blocks. The evidence is about a weekly total and is explicit that the shape of the visits does not matter.
- **Three cards set `finishBeforeSleepMin`.** Anything that deliberately turns attention toward difficult material has no business in the hour before bed.
