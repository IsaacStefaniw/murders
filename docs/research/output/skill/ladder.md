# The Skill coach's ladder

A pillar is not a list of good practices, it is an order. This pillar's
order is unusually clear because the evidence is unusually good, and
because two of its rungs are the library's only two A grades outside
`if-then-plan`.

## The learning ladder

**Rung 1 — Test yourself instead of rereading.**
`blank-page-recall` (existing, A).
Everything above this rung is an optimisation of it. Closing the book and
writing what you remember beats every control condition anyone has run it
against, and the two techniques most people actually use, highlighting and
rereading, are already named in the library's antipattern list.
*Coach line:* "Close it. Blank page. What do you remember?"

**Rung 2 — Space the testing.**
`spaced-review` (existing, A), then `let-the-gap-stretch` (new, B).
Spacing is what turns rung 1 from a study session into retention. The new
card is the dose: the gap should keep growing as an item gets solid, which
is the opposite of reviewing everything equally.
*Coach line at the transition:* "You know that one now. Push it out."

**Rung 3 — Load the front of the session.**
`guess-before-you-read` (new, C).
Only worth doing once rungs 1 and 2 are habitual, because it optimises a
session that is already happening. Three minutes of guessing before you
open the thing.

**Rung 4 — Protect the consolidation.**
`sleep-is-the-second-half` (new, C).
A scheduling constraint rather than a practice. Cross-references the
recovery pillar, which owns sleep opportunity; this rung owns *when you
practise*.

**Rung 5 — Change what you practise, not how.**
`learn-the-ground-first` (new, D), and for physical skills the interleaving
and rehearsal cards.
The last rung because it is the one people reach for first: buying another
book about technique when the actual gap is not knowing the subject.

## The habit ladder

Separate, and it feeds every other pillar rather than only this one.

1. `one-behaviour-one-cue` (new, B) — one thing, one cue, and the honest
   timeline. Two to four months for most people, and the measured spread
   runs from three weeks to most of a year.
2. `trigger-if-then` (existing, A, mind pillar) — the planning form that
   generalises.
3. `finish-on-a-good-one` (new, E) — a place to stop, honestly graded.

*Coach line at rung 1:* "Missing one day doesn't set you back. That's
measured, not me being nice."

## What the coach says when someone is stuck

**Stuck because nothing is sticking.** Check they are on rung 1 and not
rereading. It is the single most common substitution and the library names
it as an antipattern for exactly this reason.

**Stuck on a plateau in a physical skill.** Do not reach for the external
focus cue as though it were settled — this round moved it from tested to
early days on a reanalysis that found publication bias in every analysis.
It is still worth trying and the copy should say which of those it is.

**Stuck and buying planners.** Two meta-analyses say the lever is how you
feel about starting, not when you scheduled it.
