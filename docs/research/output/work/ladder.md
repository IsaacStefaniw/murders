# The Work coach's ladder

The second artefact the restructured pipeline requires. A pillar is not a
list of good practices, it is an order: what comes first, what is wasted
until the rung below is solid, and what the coach says at each transition.

This pillar has a complication no other pillar has. **The rungs differ by
whether the person controls their own hours.** Roughly half this audience
does not, and a single ladder written for a desk job would place badly
for a nurse, a chef or a driver. So there are two, and they converge.

## The desk-work ladder

**Rung 1 — The day has an end.**
`shutdown-ritual` (existing, D, proposed C) into `detachment-window`
(existing, B). Nothing above this rung works while work never finishes.
Detachment is the best-evidenced recovery variable in the pillar, and the
end-of-work plan is what hands it a clean start.
*Coach line at the transition:* "You closed it out every day this week.
That is the one everything else sits on."

**Rung 2 — The off-hours are yours.**
`days-off-are-not-saturday`, `move-after-the-hard-one`.
Once work stops, what fills the gap starts to matter, and the research
says the person choosing beats the app prescribing. Control over the
evening predicted vigour better than either detachment or relaxation did.
*Coach line:* "An evening you chose beats an evening I designed."

**Rung 3 — The meetings and the feedback stop costing so much.**
`start-at-the-minute`, `stand-for-the-short-ones`,
`cameras-off-is-fine-here`, `ten-minutes-after-the-bad-one`,
`keep-it-about-the-work`, `a-rating-is-one-persons-view`.
Cheap, mostly free, and they buy back the hours that make rungs 1 and 2
possible. Deliberately above rung 1: reclaiming meeting time is no use if
the reclaimed time goes back into work.

**Rung 4 — The job itself changes.**
`job-crafting-two-columns`, then `take-on-the-harder-thing`.
This is where the evidence gets genuinely good and where most people
never get to, because they are too depleted at rung 1 to attempt it.
*Coach line:* "You have the room now. This is the part that changes the
job rather than surviving it."

**Rung 5 — The structural conversation.**
`load-and-control-review` (existing, C), `burnout-is-not-a-personal-failing`.
Naming which of workload, control, reward, community, fairness or values
is wrong, and who can change it.

## The shift-work ladder

Same destination, different order, because the first rung is not
available. A nurse cannot decide when work ends.

**Rung 1 — The roster is visible.**
`count-the-quick-returns`, then the recovery round's sleep cards
(`night-anchor-sleep`, `night-shift-light`, `dark-glasses-home`,
`pre-nights-nap`, `on-shift-nap`, `caffeine-on-nights`).
You cannot protect the end of a day you do not control. You can see the
shape of the week and protect the sleep inside it.
*Coach line:* "Four nights this week. Here is the shape that protects the
drive home."

**Rung 2 — The dangerous edges are arranged in advance.**
`plan-the-ride-home` into the existing `no-drive-after-nights`.
Deliberately rung 2, not rung 4. This is the one with a crash on the
other side of it.

**Rung 3 — The days off are real days off.**
`days-off-are-not-saturday`, `the-break-is-for-how-you-feel`,
`heat-work-week` where the work is outdoors.
Note that "weekend" never means Saturday here, and the card is written
that way.

**Rung 4 — The roster conversation.**
`ask-for-forward-rotation`.
Forward rotation, faster rotation, a say in your own shifts. Three words,
and a number from rung 1 to bring with them.

**Rung 5 — The same structural conversation** as the desk ladder.

## The transition ladder

Separate, temporary, and every card on it carries `neverNag`. Someone
three weeks past a redundancy does not need an adherence score.

1. `the-weekday-shape` — the structure, not the search. Best-evidenced
   thing here and it asks nothing about applications.
2. `list-the-doors-once` — while it is still administrative rather than
   an admission.
3. `name-the-rejection-first` — before the first one lands.
4. `rehearse-dont-just-send` — skills and encouragement together, because
   separately neither worked.

*Coach line at rung 1:* "Start time, finish time, one reason to leave the
house. That is the whole job today."

## What the coach says when someone is stuck

**Stuck at rung 1 on the desk ladder** — they cannot make work end. Do
not offer rung 2. Go sideways to `keep-it-about-the-work` and the meeting
cards, which buy time without requiring a boundary they cannot hold yet.

**Stuck because the job is the problem.** Say so. The evidence is that
individual practice does very little when the role is unsustainable, and
pretending otherwise makes the app another voice implying they are not
trying hard enough. Naming it is respectful, not defeatist.

**Stuck on a roster they cannot change.** Say that too. Everything an app
can offer sits in the bottom two tiers of the safety hierarchy, and the
roster sits near the top where the employer is. A card that implies
otherwise is asking someone to out-practise a structural problem.

## What is deliberately not on any ladder

`accomplishment-log` (existing, D) and `holiday-lift-and-fade` are
standalone. Neither gates anything and neither is gated. The progress
principle's ranking claim is unreplicated, so the log stays a pleasant
optional card rather than a rung, and findings.md explains why it must
not be raised on the strength of a famous book.
