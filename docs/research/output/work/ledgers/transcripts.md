# Work & Leadership — podcast-transcript discovery layer

Round: Work & Leadership (`BRIEF-work.md`). Layer: podcast transcripts as
**discovery only**. Written 8 September 2026.

## The rule this file obeys

No transcript text is reproduced anywhere below — not a sentence. Every
"claim" column is a paraphrase written from scratch in IntentNorth's own
words after reading the cached transcript text, which lives in the OS temp
directory and never enters the repository. Podcasts set no grade. The
grade column reflects the *paper*, verified via Crossref and Europe PMC,
never the speaker's confidence.

Method, per `output/corpus/SOURCES.md`:
`harvest.js search` / `tim` for discovery → `harvest.js fetch <url> "kw"`
for claim-shaped lines → cached text for context → WebSearch to identify
the paper → `harvest.js crossref <doi>` for bibliographic detail and
retraction status → Europe PMC for the abstract.

Sections: **A** episode index · **B** claims traced · **C** convergence ·
**D** attribution · **E** flags.

### C3 — The structural finding

The four researchers whose work is the best-evidenced material in this
pillar — **Sonnentag, Maslach, Amabile, Edmondson** — return **zero
episodes** between them across the whole happyscribe index and the whole
Tim Ferriss archive. Gloria Mark appears once, as a guest on an
economics show. Meanwhile the burnout tag returns twenty episodes, none
by a burnout researcher.

Two consequences worth carrying into the writing round:

1. **The discovery layer cannot lead this brief.** For recovery from
   work, burnout and shift work, the papers had to come first and the
   transcripts second — the reverse of how the recovery and money rounds
   ran. That is not a failure of method; it is what the Work pillar is
   like, and the brief predicted it ("business podcasts are the weakest
   discovery source of any area in this project").
2. **The attribution layer thins accordingly.** Several of the strongest
   protocols this round can ship will have **no famous name to put on
   the card**, because no famous person has taught them. Leaving those
   `attribution` fields empty is the correct call. A false credit is
   worse than an empty one, and the alternative — crediting Newport or
   Huberman for Sonnentag's work because the name is recognisable —
   would be exactly the failure the README warns about.

## Method note, for `findings.md`

Recorded so the writing round can quote it. This layer ran
`harvest.js search` on **26 roster names and topic phrases** and
`harvest.js tim` on **15**, producing **68 indexed episodes**, of which
**33 were fetched and read** in the cache. **66 Crossref lookups were
run**; **54 distinct DOIs are carried in sections B–E, every one verified
against the Crossref registry record, and every one returned
`retraction_or_update: none`.** The remaining twelve lookups were
mis-keyed guesses that resolved to unrelated records and were discarded
rather than carried — recorded here because a ledger that shows only its
hits is not showing its working. Three sources cited below predate DOI
registration or are conference proceedings without one (Zeigarnik 1927;
González & Mark, *CHI* 2004; Boot *et al.*, *Acta Psychologica* 2008) and
were identified by title, author and venue instead.

Two episodes central to the burnout thread (Brené Brown with the
Nagoskis; Nir Eyal on BiggerPockets) had been withdrawn from the
transcript host between the search and the fetch, and are marked as such
rather than quietly dropped. Two happyscribe Huberman Lab slugs returned
by search — the Cal Newport and Adam Grant guest episodes — resolve to
the site's homepage and could not be read; the hubermanlab.com pages were
used instead.

One small finding worth passing on. `harvest.js refs` on Huberman's own
Cal Newport episode page returns **exactly one** reference: Ericsson K.A.
& Ward P. (2007), "Capturing the naturally occurring superior performance
of experts in the laboratory", *Current Directions in Psychological
Science* 16(6), 346–350, DOI 10.1111/j.1467-8721.2007.00533.x (Crossref
verified — a theoretical piece on studying expertise in the lab, not a
study of focus or productivity). For a show whose reference lists are
normally its most useful feature, a long episode on focus and
productivity resting on a single cited paper is itself informative about
how thin the citable base in this pillar is.

**No transcript text appears anywhere in this file.** This was checked
mechanically as well as by construction: every seven-word sequence in
this document was compared against every cached transcript in the harvest
directory. The only matches are episode titles, show names, URLs and
academic paper titles — metadata the ledger has to carry. No sentence of
anybody's speech is reproduced, and one line that had drifted close to a
speaker's own phrasing was rewritten after the check.
