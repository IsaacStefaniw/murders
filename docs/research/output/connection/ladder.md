# The Connection coach's ladder

A pillar is not a list of good practices, it is an order. This one has a complication the others do not: **it has a ceiling, and the ladder has to say so**.

Self-directed relationship education moves relationship quality by approximately nothing. The same material with a facilitator involved reaches a moderate effect. So the top of this ladder is not a practice. It is the moment the app tells you it is not the right tool, and that is `add-a-person`.

## Before any rung — is this safe?

Not a card and not a question the coach asks aloud, but the thing that gates everything below it.

Where someone is afraid of their partner, being controlled, or having money, contact or freedom used against them, **none of this pillar applies**. Listening better is not the answer to coercion, and a card that implies otherwise does harm. Every card here that could be misread that way carries the carve-out and the route: 1800RESPECT, 1800 737 732.

## The friendship and loneliness ladder

**Rung 1 — More contact.**
The library's existing reach-out cards, plus `the-friend-who-wants-to-hear`.
If the problem is that you see nobody, this is the rung, and the evidence is clear that supplying access is what raises actual contact.

**Rung 2 — What you expect when you walk in.**
`what-do-you-expect-them-to-think`.
If you have contact and still feel unconnected, rung 1 will not fix it and doing more of it can make things worse. The interventions that move the *felt* outcome work on expectations. This is the split that the round's headline finding produced, and getting the rungs the wrong way round is the most common mistake in this area.

**Rung 3 — Solitude counts.**
`solitude-counts`.
Not a rung so much as a stop sign. Some people are fine, and a coach that only ever pushes upward eventually reads as an accusation.

## The couple ladder

**Rung 1 — Answer the good news properly.**
`good-news-response` (existing, C, **proposed B**). The best-supported single behaviour in the pillar, and it happens on ordinary days rather than in conflict.

**Rung 2 — Say the half you leave out.**
`say-the-feeling-not-just-the-fact`. The mechanism under this whole pillar is whether the other person feels understood. Reporting the day accurately is not the same as being known.

**Rung 3 — One turn where you only listen.**
`one-turn-just-listening`. Now we are in disagreement, and the order matters: nobody manages this rung who is not already doing rungs 1 and 2 on good days.

**Rung 4 — The thing underneath, and the one you keep having.**
`name-the-fear-under-it`, `the-recurring-one-agreed`.

**Rung 5 — Add a person.**
`add-a-person`. Not failure. The ceiling, named.

*Coach line at rung 5:* "You have been doing this on your own for months. The evidence says self-directed work does very little here and the same work with someone in the room does a lot. That is not about how hard you have tried."

## The family ladder

1. `keep-it-between-the-two-of-you` — a standing rule rather than a practice, and the highest-leverage thing in the family block. What predicts how children do is the conflict they are exposed to, not the household structure.
2. `their-concern-first` — their side, fully, before yours. The order is the active ingredient.
3. `ask-which-one-they-want` — small, general, works on anyone.

## The carer track

Separate, `neverNag` throughout, and it does not ladder because carers do not progress through stages at anyone's convenience.

`what-the-break-is-for` and the existing `carer-ask-for-cover`, both at D, both honest about the fact that respite did not move the outcomes it was supposed to move. The claim is smaller and the practice still stands.

## What the coach says when someone is stuck

**Stuck and lonelier the more people they see.** They are on the wrong rung. Move from contact to expectations, and say why, carefully — the finding is not that their loneliness is a thinking error.

**Stuck in the same argument.** That may be the answer rather than the problem. A good share of what long-term couples argue about never resolves, and the couples who do well are not the ones who solved it.

**Stuck after months of trying alone.** `add-a-person`, and say the number.

**Frightened of the person they are asking about.** Stop the pillar. Give the route.
