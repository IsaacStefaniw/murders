# Connection & Family round — findings

Two coaches, one pillar. Twelve candidates, four regrades, and the safety work this pillar has been missing. Gate 1 read 53 transcripts across 50 conversations and traced 136 claims; Gate 2 opened 59 papers at registry level, every one retraction-checked.

## The ceiling held and was not approached

An integrity test forbids an A grade here and it stays. This round proposes **no A and no B**: seven C, four D, one E.

That is not a weak round, it is the honest shape of this field. Nothing about couple or family behaviour has support at the level of morning light or strength training, and the two rounds either side of this one produced A grades because their fields support them. This one does not.

## 1. The loneliness finding splits the block rather than inverting it

Gate 1 reported that the interventions that work address how a person reads social situations rather than supplying more contact, and called it an inversion of the friendship block. **Gate 2 corrected it, and the corrected version is more useful.**

The two outcomes everyone merges come apart. Across 58 randomised trials:

| Goal | What works best |
|---|---|
| More actual contact | Giving people access to others |
| Feeling less lonely | Working on what you expect from people |

So the friendship cards are not wrong, they are answering a different question from the one a lonely person is usually asking. A larger review of 280 studies confirms psychological approaches lead on the felt outcome, and grades its own confidence low. Hence C, and hence a card written very carefully: the failure mode of this entire finding is that it reads as "your loneliness is your own thinking error", and it is not.

One number Gate 1 carried is only partly verified and **must not be printed**.

## 2. The Gottman block, both sides

The prediction claims are in the papers. The design is the problem.

| Measure | Development sample | After crossvalidation |
|---|---|---|
| Accuracy | 89.7% | **69.3%** |
| Sensitivity | 91.9 | **46.0** |

At a realistic base rate the positive predictive value is about 21%. A model that identifies fewer than half the couples who will separate, and is wrong four times in five when it says a couple will, is not a prediction instrument.

Two independent counterweights the round added: one large study finding neither communication predicting satisfaction nor the reverse robustly, and a 43-dataset collaboration across 29 laboratories finding that *change* in relationship quality is largely unpredictable.

**Three widely repeated figures trace to no peer-reviewed paper at all** and are proposed for the register's list of numbers that trace to nothing. What survives is observational description of contempt and repair, which the library already carries at D, and that is where it stays.

## 3. Two claims that are smaller than advertised

**"As bad as fifteen cigarettes a day" is not in the paper.** The discussion says comparable with quitting smoking, with no cigarette count anywhere. There is also a genuine tension in the literature: one large cohort found objective isolation survived adjustment while loneliness did not. Graded C, and the pillar's strongest evidence is still not a B.

**Self-directed relationship education does approximately nothing.** Measured effect on relationship quality when delivered on your own: about 0.03, not significant. The same material with a facilitator involved reaches about 0.43.

That second one caps this entire pillar, and rather than hide it the round turned it into a card. `add-a-person` exists to tell someone that the cards have a ceiling and that reaching it is not a failure of effort. An app that never says this is selling something.

## 4. The safety work

Six live cards in this pillar carry **no safety field at all**, including two about couple behaviour, in a pillar that touches coercive control. Only 4 of 31 carry `neverNag`.

Every new card here that could be read as advice to someone being controlled carries the same carve-out and the same route, written once and shared: none of this applies where you are afraid of the person, that is not a communication problem, and 1800RESPECT is 1800 737 732.

Ten of twelve set `neverNag`. This pillar is full of things that cannot be scheduled and must never be scored: a missed week of reaching out to a friend is not a lapse, and a carer who did not take their two hours does not need a broken streak.

Recommendation carried forward: **18 of the 31 live cards should set `neverNag`, and the six with no safety field need one.**

## 6. All six Peterson cards come off

The live-library flag recorded six cards with Jordan Peterson as the sole attribution, breaching the roster's own rule. Gate 2's verdict is that **all six credits come off**, and it is stricter than Gate 1's.

Two of Gate 1's proposed replacements **failed the author-list rule as written** — the researchers it named are on none of the papers it cited. Gate 2 found the correct papers, so those two replacements can proceed. `carer-ask-for-cover` carries an empty attribution, confirmed independently against five carer meta-analyses. For one card, an empty attribution is recommended over a weak substitute.

**Applying the rule across the whole pillar turned up five further failing credits** that the original flag never covered. Two bibliography corrections were also needed, where a cited identifier resolved to a different first author than the credit assumed.

That is the author-list rule paying for itself twice over: it caught the errors it was written for, and five more nobody had looked for.

## 7. What was declined, and the shortfall admitted

The brief asked for fifteen to twenty cards. This round returns twelve.

Three of the areas it names — **teenagers, blended families, and adult sibling relationships** — produced nothing verifiable. Padding them would have been worse than the shortfall, and the contract is explicit that thin entries subtract. They are recorded as gaps rather than filled.

Also declined: prediction-accuracy claims of any kind, and anything that would have the app deliver couples therapy.

## 8. What the Connection coach can now say on a Tuesday

- "Before you go, write what you expect them to think of you. Afterwards, write what actually happened. The gap is the interesting bit."
- "They want to hear from you more than you think. That gap is biggest with the people you have been out of touch with longest."
- "Take one turn where your only job is to understand. Not agreeing. Understanding."
- "Ask which one they want before you answer. Four words, and it removes the guess."
- "That argument is a standing difference, not a problem you are failing to solve. Most long-term couples have several."
- "No child carries a message. What predicts how kids do is the conflict they are exposed to, not whether you are together."
- "Two hours that are yours. They do not have to fix anything."
- "You have been doing this alone for months. The next step is not another exercise, it is a third person in the room, and that is not failure."

## 9. Product notes

- **Six live cards need a safety field**, two of them about couple behaviour.
- **`neverNag` should go from 4 cards to 18.**
- **The violence carve-out should be a shared constant**, not retyped per card. This round uses one.
- **`add-a-person` should be reachable from anywhere in the pillar**, not only on its own schedule. It is the card that tells someone the app has a ceiling, and the people who most need it are the ones deepest into the other cards.
