## The Gottman block, verified

| Claim | Status |
|---|---|
| Development-sample accuracy 89.71% | In the paper |
| **After crossvalidation: 69.31%** | Heyman RE, Smith Slep AM. 2001, *The Hazards of Predicting Divorce Without Crossvalidation*, J Marriage Fam 63(2):473–479, PMC1622921, open access, read in full |
| Sensitivity 91.89 falls to **45.95** | Same |
| Positive predictive value at a realistic 16% base rate: **20.99%** | Same |
| "93% accuracy" | Gottman & Levenson 2000's own abstract does say it. The number is in the papers; the design is the problem |
| The 33% and 86% bids figures | **Trace to no peer-reviewed paper.** Proposed for the register's numbers-that-trace-to-nothing list |
| The 69% perpetual-problems figure | **Same** |

Counterweights the round added: Lavner JA, Karney BR, Bradbury TN. 2016 — neither communication predicting satisfaction nor the reverse is robust. Joel S et al. 2020, PNAS — 43 datasets across 29 laboratories, with *change* in relationship quality largely unpredictable.

The peer-reviewed bids paper is Driver & Gottman 2004: n=49, observational, with the authors themselves disclaiming causation. Bids stays **D**. Prediction is **E and does not ship**.

## Two other claims checked

**"As bad as 15 cigarettes a day."** Holt-Lunstad JT et al. 2010's discussion says "comparable with quitting smoking". **There is no cigarette count in the paper.** A genuine tension also exists in this literature: Steptoe A et al. 2013 (ELSA, n=6,500) found objective isolation survived adjustment (HR 1.26) while loneliness did not (HR 0.92). This is the pillar's strongest evidence and it is still a C.

## Bibliography corrections

Two identifiers used in this round's inputs resolved to a different first author than the credit assumed. Both were corrected before anything shipped, and both are the same shape as the traps already in `../corpus/RETRACTIONS.md`: **check the DOI, never the author-year.**
