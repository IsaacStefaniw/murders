# Verified source ledger: shift work sleep, jet lag, consumer sleep wearables, HRV

Compiled 2026-09-08. Verification method: every citation below was checked against its Europe PMC mirror of the PubMed record (title, authors, journal, year, PMID/DOI, abstract) and, where available, the publisher/PMC landing page. PubMed's own HTML refuses non-cookie fetches, so the Europe PMC REST endpoint (same record, same PMID) was used for the bibliographic check; the DOI/PMID links given resolve to the paper. Anything not fully confirmed is marked UNVERIFIED. Vendor pages were read on 2026-09-08 and dated where the page shows a date.

Grades apply to the study, never the speaker. Taxonomy: A = meta-analyses and broad agreement; B = controlled trials, or very large consistent cohorts; C = smaller, narrower or mixed studies; D = what experienced people do ahead of research; E = unproven.

Note on the brief: the "Minors & Waterhouse 1981/1983 Ergonomics or Chronobiologia" citation does not exist under those venues. The two indexed anchor-sleep papers are Int J Chronobiol 1981 and J Physiol 1983 (below). Their subject numbers are not in the abstracts and the full texts are paywalled; n is UNVERIFIED.

### Section 1 effect-size digest
- Anchor sleep: qualitative (period 24 h vs >24 h); n unknown.
- Light on shift: DLMO/temperature delays ~9-11 h vs ~4-5 h in controls over ~12 shifts (Boivin 2002, n=19); near-complete re-entrainment after 5 simulated shifts (Crowley 2003, n=67); Cochrane pooled sleepiness -0.83 SSS points, very low quality.
- Naps: end-of-shift sleepiness SMD 0.40 (Martin-Gill 2018); reaction time unchanged; 30-min nap carries measurable inertia to 47 min, 10-min does not (Hilditch 2016, n=31); 30-min 04:00 nap in 109 nurses improved sleepiness and cognition 05:00-07:00 (Zion 2019).
- Nap + caffeine before the first nights: alertness improved on MWT/PVT (Schweitzer 2006, lab 68 / field 53); Cochrane KSS -0.63 (low).
- Drive home: 37.5% of post-shift drives had near-crashes vs 0% (Lee 2016, n=16).
- Full adaptation: <3% complete, <25% useful (Folkard 2008).

### Section 2 plain statement
The rule "light in the morning after eastward travel, avoid it before your body's temperature minimum" is B-grade physiology (PRC) and B-grade in randomised pre-flight lab trials measured by DLMO. Its translation into travel itineraries is D: expert-designed, never tested as a package in a field RCT, and the one systematic review of field studies (Bin 2019) found 9 of 13 negative. Practical numbers: expect ~1 h/day advance and up to ~2 h/day delay with timed light; pre-shift 1 h/day (east) or 2 h/day (west) for 2-3 days; beyond ~8 zones eastward, consider delaying instead (wrong-way risk).

### Section 3 plain statements
- Sleep versus wake: good. Sensitivity to sleep is 91-97% in every study and generation. Total sleep time is usually within ~10-30 min of PSG in healthy sleepers.
- Wake: poor. Specificity for wake is 0.18-0.54 (Chinoy 2021), 29-52% (Schyvens 2025), 48% (Oura Gen1), 51% (Whoop 3.0). Roughly half of true wake epochs within the night are scored as sleep, so wearables under-count wake after sleep onset and over-state sleep efficiency, and they are worst on fragmented nights — exactly the nights a user cares about.
- Stages: fair-to-moderate at best. Epoch kappa 0.20-0.65 by device and population. Deep-sleep epoch hit rate ~47-80% (Apple S8 ~50%; Oura Gen3 ~80% in Robbins 2024 but kappa only 0.35 in Lee 2023). The best result in the literature — Oura Gen3 nightly stage totals not statistically different from PSG (Robbins 2024) — still corresponds to a kappa of 0.65 at the epoch level. Night-to-night stage numbers should be read as trends, never as a measurement of deep sleep.

## Contradicted or unsupported practices
1. "Sleep straight after the night shift, every time, no matter what" — not contradicted, but the fixed-anchor evidence (C) is weaker than its popularity; consistency of a ~4 h anchor block is what the data support, not any particular clock time.
2. Two naps per night shift — Cochrane: statistically non-significant increase in sleepiness (very low quality). Prefer one nap.
3. 30-min-plus naps immediately before safety-critical tasks — lab inertia to at least 47 min (Hilditch 2016). Either 10-min naps or a 30-45 min recovery buffer.
4. Low HRV reading = overtrained / take the day off — contradicted by Bellenger 2016 (RMSSD rises in overreaching) and by the dominance of alcohol/illness effects.
5. Trusting "deep sleep minutes" as a measurement — contradicted by every PSG validation.
6. Using Apple Health HRV to compare with Oura/WHOOP HRV — different statistics (SDNN vs rMSSD), different sampling; vendors deliberately do not sync HRV.
7. Wearable "illness detection" as an early-warning system — specificity 63% (Oura), 34% of symptomatic negatives flagged (WHOOP); PPV unreported by either.
8. Driving home after a night shift as routine — 37.5% near-crash rate in 2 h drives (Lee 2016).
9. Large caffeine (400 mg) within 8-12 h of the day sleep — disrupts PSG-measured sleep (Gardiner 2025; Drake 2013). 100 mg did not.
10. Expecting to "adapt" to permanent nights — <3% do.

| Practice | Shape | Grade | Basis |
|---|---|---|---|
