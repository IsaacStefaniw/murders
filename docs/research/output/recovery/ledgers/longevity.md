# Longevity behaviours: verified source ledger (final, 2026-09-08)

Verification method: every PMID was pulled from the NCBI E-utilities API (esearch -> efetch: title, first author, journal, year, DOI, abstract, and CommentsCorrections/PublicationType fields for errata and retractions) and, where noted, cross-checked against Europe PMC (verbatim abstract), Crossref (metadata and update-to/updated-by fields), ClinicalTrials.gov API, and the publisher or PMC page. PubMed search pages returned a cookie wall and PubPeer returned HTTP 403 to the fetcher; API records were used instead. "VERIFIED" = record opened and title/author/year/journal/design/n confirmed. Anything not confirmed from a primary record is marked UNVERIFIED.

Grading taxonomy (grade the study, never the speaker): A = meta-analyses and broad agreement across many studies; B = controlled trials, or very large consistent cohorts followed for years; C = smaller, narrower or mixed; D = practitioner practice ahead of research; E = unproven. Cohort evidence, however large, is reported as "associated with", never "causes".

Podcasts (The Drive, Huberman Lab, FoundMyFitness) were used for discovery only and are not cited as evidence.

## 3. Overclaims checked

| Claim | Verdict | What the verified evidence actually supports |
|---|---|---|
| "VO2 max is the single best predictor of longevity" | Overclaim (partly true). | CRF is among the strongest single predictors in the cohorts that measured it: Kokkinos 2022 found being unfit "carried a greater risk than any of the cardiac risk factors examined"; Mandsager 2018 found low fitness compared unfavourably with smoking, diabetes and CAD. But (a) both are treadmill-estimated METs in referred clinical samples, not measured VO2max in the general population; (b) "single best" has never been tested head-to-head against every competitor (age, frailty indices, multimorbidity, socioeconomic position, grip strength) in one model; (c) it is a predictor, i.e. a marker that also absorbs subclinical disease and lifestyle. Correct phrasing: "one of the strongest modifiable predictors of mortality in large cohorts". |
| "Grip strength causes longevity" | Unsupported as causal. | Grip strength is associated with mortality (Leong 2015 HR 1.16 per 5 kg lower; Lopez-Bueno 2022 meta-analysis of 3.1M). No trial shows raising grip strength lowers mortality, and no Mendelian-randomisation study of grip-on-mortality was found (MR work to date covers grip vs BMD, CVD, CRF). Grip is a cheap marker of overall muscle and health status, partly reflecting illness that has already started (4-year follow-up in PURE). What IS supported: resistance training itself is associated with 10-27% lower mortality (Momma 2022; Shailendra 2022). Correct phrasing: "low grip strength is associated with higher mortality; strength training is associated with lower mortality". |
| "Sitting is the new smoking" | False. | Vallance 2018: sedentary-behaviour all-cause HR 1.22 vs smoking RR 2.8 (heavy smokers 4.1-4.4); 190 vs >2,000 excess deaths per 100,000 per year. Biswas 2015 HR 1.24. Sitting's association is real but roughly an order of magnitude smaller than smoking's, and it is largely attenuated by 30-40 min/day of MVPA (Ekelund 2020) or by meeting 150 min/week (Stamatakis 2019); smoking harm is not offset by exercise. |
| "Older adults need double the protein" | Overclaim. | PROT-AGE and ESPEN recommend 1.0-1.2 g/kg/day for healthy older adults (25-50% above the 0.8 g/kg RDA), 1.2-1.5 for illness, up to 2.0 only for severe illness/injury/malnutrition. These are grade-C consensus figures. Nunes 2022 (RCT meta-analysis) finds the lean-mass benefit in >=65s at 1.2-1.59 g/kg/day with resistance training. "Double" (1.6 g/kg) is the plateau from young/trained RT populations in Morton 2018, not an older-adult requirement. No trial links any protein target to mortality in older adults. |
| "Walk 10,000 steps a day" | Not evidence-based as a threshold. | Paluch 2022 (15 cohorts, 47,471): mortality risk falls progressively then flattens at 6,000-8,000 steps/day for >=60 y and 8,000-10,000 for <60 y. Lee 2019 (16,741 older women): plateau ~7,500. 10,000 is not harmful, simply not where the curve turns; the largest gains are from ~3,500 to ~6,000-7,000. Step intensity adds little once volume is accounted for. |
| "There is an upper limit / too much fitness is harmful" (the inverse overclaim) | Not supported. | Mandsager: elite vs high HR 0.77, "no observed upper limit of benefit". Kokkinos: "no evidence of an increase in risk with extremely high CRF." Data are thin at the extreme tail, so absence of harm is a bounded statement, not proof of unlimited benefit. |

## 4. Contradicted or unsupported practices

| Practice | Status | Evidence |
|---|---|---|
| Standing desks as a cardiovascular or longevity intervention | Contradicted for CVD; supported only for reducing sitting minutes | Shrestha 2018 Cochrane: sit-stand desks cut sitting ~100 min/day short-term (low-quality evidence), no health endpoints. Ahmadi 2024 (83,013, thigh accelerometer): standing time not associated with major CVD and associated with higher orthostatic circulatory disease above ~2 h/day. Standing is not "activity"; the offset evidence is for MVPA (Ekelund 2020: 30-40 min/day). |
| High-volume resistance training for longevity (more is better) | Mixed / unsupported above ~60 min/week | Momma 2022 and Shailendra 2022 both show the mortality association peaking at ~30-60 min/week with a J-shaped decline at higher self-reported volumes; the upper arm is grade C (sparse data) but there is no evidence that more than ~60 min/week of strengthening buys more mortality benefit. Higher volumes are still supported for other goals (hypertrophy, strength). |
| Low-protein diet in midlife to extend life (from Levine 2014) | Unsupported | Single 24-h recall cohort (6,381), ~21 diabetes deaths driving the "5-fold" claim, formally criticised, not replicated as an age crossover. Grade C. Do not translate into an age-specific protein prescription. |
| Protein above ~1.6 g/kg/day for extra muscle (healthy adults doing RT) | Contradicted for additional benefit | Morton 2018 (49 RCTs): no further gains above ~1.6 g/kg/day; Nunes 2022 consistent. Not a safety finding; a "no further benefit" finding. |
| Sleeping longer to live longer | Unsupported | Cappuccio 2010: long sleep carries the larger mortality RR (1.30) but this is almost certainly partly reverse causation; there is no trial of sleep extension on mortality. |
| Rapamycin, metformin, NMN/NR, resveratrol for lifespan | Unproven (E) | See section 2. No positive outcome-level human trial exists for any of them. |
| "Offsetting" sitting with 60-75 min/day of exercise (the specific number) | Superseded | Self-report figure (Ekelund 2016) halved to 30-40 min/day when accelerometers were used (Ekelund 2020). |
