# HEAT / SAUNA — verified source ledger

Compiled 2026-09-08. Verification method: each paper's landing page (publisher full text, Europe PMC / PubMed record via the Europe PMC REST API, or PMC full text) was fetched and title / first author / year / journal / design / n confirmed against the page or abstract. "Verified" = the record was opened and the numbers below were read from it. "UNVERIFIED" = record could not be opened; not leaned on. PubMed's own HTML was cookie-walled to the fetcher, so PubMed records were read through Europe PMC (same MEDLINE data, same PMIDs).

Grading: A = meta-analyses / broad agreement across many studies; B = controlled trials, or very large consistent cohorts followed for years; C = smaller, narrower or mixed studies, mostly observational; D = experienced practice ahead of research; E = unproven. The study is graded, never the speaker. Podcasts were used only as a discovery aid for paper names; none are cited.

**Section 1 summary.** Every load-bearing cohort finding comes from one study population (KIHD, Eastern Finland), with sauna habits measured once by questionnaire in the 1980s or late 1990s. Effects are consistent in direction across outcomes, which is why the block sits at the top of C rather than lower, but same-cohort consistency is not independent replication. No KIHD paper can show causation, the authors say so in each paper, and two published letters in the same journal say it more sharply.

**Section 2 summary.** The controlled-trial base is small: one 20-person immersion trial with striking vascular results (Brunt), a 47-person RCT where 15 min of post-exercise sauna added ~8 mmHg SBP and ~2.7 mL/kg/min estimated VO2max over exercise alone (Lee 2022), a 41-person Finnish-sauna RCT in CAD that found nothing on FMD, PWV or BP (Debray 2023), and a 20-RCT meta-analysis whose pooled effects are mostly null with a possible 2–4 mmHg SBP reduction for whole-body heating (Hamaya 2025). Acute physiology (HR to ~light-exercise levels, transient BP fall, eHSP70 rise, plasma-volume expansion) is reproducible but is C-level mechanistic work.

**Can the KIHD cohort findings be extended to infrared cabins?** No, on the evidence. (1) KIHD participants used Finnish sauna at a mean 78.9 °C for a mean 14 min; the dementia paper explicitly says the results "cannot be directly applied to steam rooms", and the same logic applies to 40–60 °C infrared cabins. (2) The infrared trial base is 25 mostly small studies dominated by clinical Waon work at 60 °C in heart-failure patients, plus acute crossovers of 10–16 healthy people. (3) The one head-to-head-ish comparison (Hussain 2022, n=10) found infrared produced a thermoregulatory response without the cardiovascular loading of exercise. (4) Hussain & Cohen 2018 state outright that the evidence cannot yet distinguish Finnish from infrared effects — which cuts both ways: there is no evidence infrared is *worse*, but there is no cohort or trial evidence that infrared confers the KIHD associations. Marketing claims of a "2024 Eur J Prev Cardiol meta-analysis of 17 infrared studies / 2,264 participants" and a "2023 JACC RCT of 89 CAD patients on infrared sauna" appeared only on a sauna-retailer blog; no such papers were found in Europe PMC or on the journals' sites, and the real Eur J Prev Cardiol sauna paper is Lee 2018 (Finnish sauna, n=102, single session). Treat those two citations as **fabricated until someone produces a DOI**.

## Contradicted or unsupported practices

1. **Sauna as a substitute for exercise.** Contradicted by Kunutsor 2018 (fitness HR 0.51 vs sauna 0.74), Hussain 2022 (no exercise-like response), and Hamaya 2025 (no lipid/HR/HRV effect).
2. **Post-workout sauna for muscle growth.** Contradicted by Ahokas 2025 (n=40 null), Stadnyk 2018, Labidi 2021; the one positive local-heat RCT (Nakamura 2019) used heat *before* low-intensity training in untrained arms.
3. **Sauna for "detox".** Unsupported (Genuis 2011 shows detectability only).
4. **Alcohol in or around the sauna.** Contradicted by Kenttämies 2008 (alcohol in 50% of Finnish sauna deaths), Yang 2018 (79%), Hannuksela 2001 (explicit warning).
5. **Sauna in the first trimester as a heat-load practice.** Contraindicated on the Moretti 2005 hyperthermia meta-analysis (OR 1.92) and Milunsky 1992 combined-heat signal; do not present customary Finnish practice as overriding this.
6. **Sauna with unstable angina, recent MI, severe aortic stenosis.** Contraindicated (Hannuksela 2001).
7. **Treating infrared cabins as evidentially interchangeable with 80 °C Finnish sauna.** Unsupported (Sections 5 and 7).
8. **Sauna immediately before bed as a sleep aid.** Unsupported for sauna; the bath data say 1–2 h *before* bed, and sauna's larger heat load argues for at least that gap.
9. **Sauna for heart failure (Waon).** Clinical therapy, not a practice; out of scope.
10. **Anyone on strong anticholinergics, non-selective beta-blockers, adrenaline or anti-Parkinson drugs treating sauna as risk-free.** Hospers 2024 shows measurable core-temperature impairment; medical advice first.

## Practices the evidence supports, with the scheduling shape

Grades are the grade of the *best supporting study*, not the confidence in the practice.

| practice | shape | grade | source |
|---|---|---|---|
| Regular traditional (≈75–90 °C) sauna as part of a healthy lifestyle | **2–3 sessions/week** is where every KIHD outcome shows its first signal; **4–7/week** is where the point estimates are largest but the strata are small (201 men in 2015; CI 0.08–0.65 in 2018). Say "2 or more sessions a week"; do not promise the 4–7×/wk numbers. | **C** | Laukkanen 2015, 2018; Zaccardi 2017; Kunutsor 2017, 2018 |
| Session length | **≥ ~15–20 min per session** at Finnish temperatures: the 2015 paper's >19-min group had lower fatal CVD (HR 0.76) and SCD (0.48), and the cohort mean was 14 min; duration had **no** association with all-cause mortality, so do not sell "longer = live longer". | **C** | Laukkanen 2015 |
| Sauna added on top of exercise, not instead of it | Fitness dominates; exercise + sauna beat exercise alone for SBP and estimated VO2max in one small RCT. | **C** | Kunutsor 2018; Lee 2022 |
| Post-endurance-training sauna for plasma-volume / heat adaptation | ~30 min at ~87–105 °C, **immediately after** training, 3–4×/week for ~3 weeks (10–13 sessions); expect plasma volume +7–18% and better heat tolerance; performance gain ~2% in one n=6 study; not shown for cycling. | **C** | Scoon 2007; Stanley 2015; Kirby 2021; Ahokas 2025 review |

- **Europe PMC comment/correction lists** on the five load-bearing records: Laukkanen 2015 has six "Comment in" entries (the JAMA IM letters above and a Nat Rev Cardiol note) — commentary, no correction or retraction; Brunt 2016 has eight J Physiol commentary entries — no correction; BMC Med 2018, Haghayegh 2019, Scoon 2007: none.
- **Retraction Watch** site search for "sauna": "Nothing Found". Domain-restricted web search of retractionwatch.com / retractiondatabase.org for the load-bearing authors: no relevant results (only unrelated retraction notices surfaced).
- **PubPeer**: direct search page returned HTTP 403 to the fetcher; domain-restricted web search of pubpeer.com for the authors + sauna returned **no links**. Reported as "nothing found, with the caveat that PubPeer could not be queried directly".
- **Replication status**: the KIHD mortality/dementia/stroke/hypertension associations have **not been replicated in any independent cohort** as of this search; the two "men and women" papers (BMC Med 2018, Neurology 2018) are the same KIHD re-examination sample. Published critiques exist (Kivimäki et al. 2015; Epstein & Shoenfeld 2015). The strongest recent trial-level tests of the postulated mechanism at KIHD-like dose (Debray 2023, 4×/wk at 79 °C, n=41) and the pooled RCT literature (Hamaya 2025) are null-to-modest.
