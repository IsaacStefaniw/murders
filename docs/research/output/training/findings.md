# Training round — findings

Eight candidates, none graded up, and one regrade that took the recovery round's last A grade away. The brief calls this the coach Isaac rates highest and says the job is depth and honesty rather than rescue. That is what this is.

## 1. The regrade is the round's most important output

`strength-minimum-weekly` goes from **A to B**, and its number survives. The full working is in `../recovery/findings.md`, because the card lives in that round. The short version:

A 2026 cohort of 147,374 people reports a mortality optimum of 90 to 120 minutes a week in its abstract, against the card's 30 to 60. Opening the full text recovered the whole curve: hazard ratios of 0.95, 0.91, 0.91, 0.87 and **0.92** across the ascending bands. Almost all the benefit arrives by the first half hour, the middle bands overlap heavily, and the top band being *worse* than the one below marks the upper end as noise. The cancer-mortality benefit appears only in the two lowest bands.

**So the prescription stays and the grade goes.** The A rested on an agreement between pooled analyses that no longer exists, one supporting figure came from a four-study arm, and everything here is self-reported while a B-graded card in the same round rests on device-measured data. A two-hour ceiling was added and a sentence removed.

Finding our own A grade overstated, by checking it rather than by being told, is the pipeline working.

## 2. The six-voice convergence is two and a half evidence bases

Discovery found six independent communicators landing on roughly an hour a week across two sessions, from what looked like four different evidence bases. Verified separately:

| Base | Weight it actually carries |
|---|---|
| Prospective cohorts | Carries the finding |
| Volume meta-regressions | Second, and consistent |
| The minimum-training review | Third, and much smaller than its title |
| Coaching practice | **None** |

The practical answer is unchanged, which is worth saying: an hour a week across two sessions is a good instruction. But convergence counted four bases where there are two and a half, and one of the four was people agreeing with each other.

## 3. The review the brief most wanted is smaller than its title

The brief's number-one topic is the least training that still works, and there is a review addressing it directly with a title that promises exactly that. Verified: **six studies, a single-reviewer search, pooled within-group before-and-after with no control groups, men only**, and a confidence interval on the bench press running from 0.68 to 15.83 kilograms.

Its authors are honest about it. Their phrase is **"suboptimal, yet significant"**, and `one-set-week` uses that phrase, because it is the encouraging framing and the accurate one at the same time. Graded C, and it is still the most useful new card in the round.

## 4. Zone 2, verified carefully rather than dismissively

As a phrase it returns essentially nothing in the meta-analytic literature outside elite training-intensity reviews. A fourteen-expert panel could not agree on a definition until 2025 and states that the adaptations are probably **not unique** to that intensity. A 2025 review finds the optimality claim unsupported, though it is a narrative review with an interested author, so it shows the claim is unsupported rather than that intervals win.

**What survives.** Easy aerobic work is genuinely good for you and the practice keeps its B. What comes off is the branding, the mitochondrial mechanism as stated, and the watch zone as though it were a measured thing.

The best output from all of this is a rule with three independent routes to it: **when the week is short, intensity is what to protect.**

## 5. The strongest evidence in the pillar has zero podcast coverage

Preventing falls in people over sixty-five: **219 randomised trials, 167,864 participants**, a network meta-analysis conducted for a national task force. Nothing in the corpus discusses it.

Three findings inside it make the card warmer than the topic sounds. Balance and resistance work was the format participants **preferred**. Doing it **individually rather than in a group** was preferred with high confidence, so home is the version people want. And **adding more components did not add benefit**, so nobody needs a programme.

`steady-on-your-feet` is the best card in this round and it ships with an empty attribution.

## 6. A debunk we decided not to write

Discovery proposed retiring static stretching before lifting. Verification found it had pointed at the paper on *chronic* stretching rather than the *acute* one.

The right paper covers 83 studies and 2,012 participants, finds an effect of −0.21, a meaningful loss **only at holds of 60 seconds or more**, and **nothing at all on jumping or sprinting**. Its authors explicitly reject excluding stretching from warm-ups.

So the honest card is not a debunk. It is "long holds cost a little, short ones cost nothing, keep your warm-up". Recorded as the round's clearest near-miss, and the reason time-back items get verified like everything else.

## 7. Attribution

Eight of eight cards ship with an empty attribution.

The author-list rule was applied to every proposed credit. **Eight researcher credits pass.** Two fail and must not be written: one communicator who is on no cited paper's author list, and one researcher credited on a paper he did not author. Both were proposed in good faith by discovery and both would have looked authoritative.

## 8. What the Training coach can now say on a Tuesday

- "The week fell apart. One hard set each of a squat, a press and a pull. Fifteen minutes. The researchers' own words are suboptimal yet significant, which is exactly right."
- "Balance and resistance, two or three times a week, for three months. It is the best-evidenced thing in this whole pillar and people preferred doing it at home."
- "You do no formal exercise, so let's not start there. Three hard minutes scattered through the day."
- "When you think you have two left, you probably have three."
- "If strength is the goal you do not need to go to failure. Stopping two short gets the same strength and leaves you able to train tomorrow."
- "Short week? Protect the hard sessions, not the easy ones."
