# The Nutrition coach's ladder

A pillar is not a list of good practices, it is an order. This one has a particular hazard: nutrition is the easiest place in the library to make someone feel watched, and a ladder that starts with measurement will lose the people it most needs to reach.

So it starts with the form of the food rather than the amount, and no rung asks anyone to count anything.

## Rung 1 — The same food, one step back

`less-processed-same-food` (**B**).

The best-evidenced single lever here and the only card in the round at B. People given matched diets ate five hundred more calories a day on the processed version without choosing to. That makes it a card about the shopping list rather than about willpower, and it is the right first rung because it changes nothing about how much or when.

*Coach line:* "Same list, one step less processed. You are not choosing worse on the other one. The food is doing it."

## Rung 2 — Fullness without restriction

`the-version-that-takes-chewing` (**C**).

Still no amounts and still no counting. Food that takes longer to eat leaves people fuller for the same energy. Second rung because it compounds with the first and asks nothing new of anyone.

## Rung 3 — When, for people whose day is unusual

`eat-in-the-daylight` (**C**) for night workers, and the existing `kitchen-closed` with the last-bite framing this round proposes.

Deliberately not rung 1, because meal timing is where this field is loudest and its evidence is much smaller than its volume. **And the popular mechanism is wrong.** The trial usually invoked found no difference in energy expenditure, resting metabolic rate or weight lost, only in hunger. The reason to eat earlier is appetite and overnight glucose, not calorie burning, and any copy saying otherwise should be corrected.

## Rung 4 — The specific goals

`protein-before-bed-on-lifting-days` (**C**) if training. `the-cholesterol-plate` (**C**) if that is the number being worked on. `what-you-eat-changes-the-night` (**C**) if sleep is the problem and the evening has already been tried.

Last rung because each is for a particular person, and offering all of them to everyone is how a nutrition coach becomes noise.

## Running alongside

`plan-food-after-a-short-night` (**C**) is not a rung. It fires the morning after bad sleep, and it exists to say that the hunger is physiology rather than a failure of yours.

## What the coach says when someone is stuck

**Asking for a protein number.** Give a range, never a point. The figure everyone quotes comes from a paper whose own analysis does not support it and whose discussion argues for considerably more. Most people should eat more protein than they do, and nobody has located the ceiling.

**Asking whether to space protein across meals.** They do not need to. The per-meal ceiling and the amino-acid threshold underneath it were both looked for this round and neither was found.

**Worried about water.** Below about two per cent of body mass there is no measurable effect on thinking. Drink when thirsty.

**Trying to fix everything at once.** Rung 1 only. The pillar's existing `one-lever-only` card holds its A grade and gained a much better source this round, and it exists for exactly this moment.

## What this ladder deliberately does not do

It never asks anyone to weigh, count or log. Two cards carry an explicit eating-disorder off-ramp, and any future card in this pillar touching amounts, counting or restriction should carry one too.
