**Roughly a third of the nutrition slice is gut-microbiome content**, and
almost all of it is on venues whose guest sells a probiotic, a test kit or
a protocol book. That is the single most product-captured area in the
whole corpus and it is flagged throughout section F.

**The aggregator is not where this round's evidence lives.** As
`SOURCES.md` predicted, the strongest nutrition and training voices —
Danny Lennon, Nuckols, Trexler, Helms, Carvalho, Luc van Loon, Stuart
Phillips, Brad Schoenfeld — are either not on the aggregator at all or are
researchers who do not tour. The direct sites carried this round.

## C. CONVERGENCE

Convergence sets the order of verification. It never sets a grade and it
is never evidence. What follows counts **independent** voices — people
reasoning from different literatures, not people repeating each other.

### Genuine convergence — verify these first

**1. A strength floor of roughly an hour a week, in two sessions, holds
almost everything. — 6 independent voices. VERIFY FIRST.**
Luc van Loon (two sessions protect muscle even in a calorie deficit),
Mike Israetel (an hour a week in two or three twenty-minute sessions
transforms a beginner), Abbie Smith-Ryan (as time shrinks, protect
intensity; two resistance days plus two or three aerobic), Andy Galpin
(at least once a week in a deficit; design at a fifth of the person's
energy budget), Greg Nuckols (strength plateaus at a low weekly volume
even where size does not), and the FoundMyFitness reading of a
147,374-person cohort. **Genuine**: these rest on tracer studies, volume
meta-regressions, prospective cohorts and coaching practice respectively —
four different evidence bases reaching one number. This is the round's
strongest candidate and the brief's stated priority.

**2. Exercise is a weight-maintenance lever, not a weight-loss lever.
— 4 independent voices. VERIFY FIRST.**
Daniel Lieberman (the arithmetic, plus the Boston police trial where only
the continuing exercisers kept it off), Mike Israetel (the same arithmetic
from a lifting perspective), Andy Galpin (the deficit is nutrition, the
muscle is training), Layne Norton (NEAT explains much of the variance).
**Genuine**, and unusually valuable because it is the opposite of what
most fitness marketing says, and because it removes a common reason people
quit.

**3. There is no anabolic window in the popular sense. — 4 voices.**
van Loon (sensitivity still raised at the next morning's breakfast),
Galpin (timing matters only when sessions are close together), Israetel,
Norton. **Genuine**, and it is already named as a time-back target in the
training brief.

**4. Eat earlier rather than later. — 4 independent lines, with one
important dissent.**
St-Onge (delayed meals reduce fat oxidation; timing predicted weight-loss
success), Sigma's Flanagan and Lennon (the circadian appetite mechanism,
seven named papers), FoundMyFitness (early windows improved insulin and
blood pressure independently of weight), van Loon (a caution about the
late-window version). **Genuine on the physiology.** The dissent is ZOE's
community study: people find it far easier to start later than to stop
earlier, and left alone they drift late. **The metabolically better
version is the harder version to adhere to**, and the library's whole
premise says adherence wins. That tension is the most interesting thing in
the nutrition half of this round and belongs in the ladder, not buried in
a card.

**5. Short vigorous bouts are worth something. — 4 voices.**
FoundMyFitness (stair-climbing trial, sitting-interruption crossover),
Smith-Ryan (sub-maximal intervals still work, just slower), Galpin (one
hard session a week), the VILPA cohorts. **Genuine on fitness and
glucose**; the mortality half is entirely observational and in
non-exercisers, and the advocate's own page says so.

**6. Wide rep ranges all build muscle when taken near failure. — 4
voices.** Israetel, Nuckols, van Loon, and Attia by implication when he
explains why he trains at eight to twelve rather than one to five.
**Genuine**, and it is what makes band and bodyweight training defensible.

**7. Sleep loss shows up on the plate. — 3 voices.**
St-Onge (the whole research programme), Galpin, Kristen Holmes.
**Genuine but concentrated**: St-Onge is the source, and the other two are
relaying her field. Count it as one and a half.

### False or fragile convergence — the useful findings

**8. "1.6 grams per kilo" as a settled number. — 5 voices, ONE paper,
and the paper does not say it.**
Rhonda Patrick, Peter Attia, Layne Norton, Andy Galpin and most of the
field state this figure. Every route leads to the same meta-analysis
(Morton et al., 2018). Greg Nuckols read that paper's actual breakpoint
analysis and found the breakpoint **is not statistically significant**,
that the confidence interval around it spans nearly the whole range of
intakes analysed, and that supplementary figures in the same paper point
higher. **This is textbook false convergence** — five credible independent
people, one source, and a misread of it — and it is exactly what
`COMMUNICATORS.md` warned convergence could be. The direction (more than
the RDA, paired with lifting) survives. The number must not be written as
though it were established.

**9. Creatine for cognition. — 2 enthusiastic voices, 1 sceptic on the
same episode, and a literature that says no.**
Patrick advances the sleep-deprivation and Alzheimer's pilots; Attia, in
the same conversation, says they are too small to lean on; and a 2024
review in Behavioural Brain Research concludes that the research fails to
support the theoretical basis for a cognitive effect at all. **Fragile.**
The muscle case for creatine is unaffected and remains A-grade. The brain
case should not be written as more than early.

**10. Ultra-processed food causes overeating. — 3 voices, one causal
trial.**
Norton and Patrick both describe Hall's 2019 inpatient randomised trial
identically and correctly, and a BMJ umbrella review of the epidemiology
sits behind them. But the *causal* claim rests on that single small
four-week trial. **Genuine as a direction, thin as a mechanism**, and the
honest card says the epidemiology is enormous and the experiment is one
study.

**11. Creatine loading is unnecessary. — 3 voices agreeing on the
practice and disagreeing on the facts.**
Patrick says saturation takes three to four weeks without loading;

means several of this round's best cards will ship with a researcher-only
