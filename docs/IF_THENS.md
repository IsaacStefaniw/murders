# The three if-thens

`trigger-if-then` — **evidence level A**, the highest grade in this
app. Gollwitzer and Sheeran. *"Around ninety-four studies pooled to a
medium-to-large effect, working hardest on the intentions people keep
failing to act on, because the decision is already made before the
moment arrives."*

Of 323 protocols, 15 are grade A. This is one of them, and it is the
one aimed squarely at what you are doing.

---

## The two rules that rule out most of what people write

Both are from the protocol's own text, and both matter more than the
wording:

**1. Name a real situation, not a vague day.** *"The fourth round at
the same table, the phone at 11pm."* Not "when I'm stressed." A
situation you would recognise from the outside.

**2. The "then" must be something you DO, not something you withhold.**
*"Plans written as 'I will not' leave you nothing to do with the
moment."* This is why "I won't vape tonight" is not an if-then. It is
an intention, which is the thing you already had.

And the reason the frame matters at all, in the protocol's words:
*"the claim that self-control is a fuel tank that empties has failed
large multi-lab replication, so any plan whose active ingredient is
wanting it more is not a plan."*

---

## One — Monday, walking in from futsal

**This is the one that is now evidenced rather than predicted.**

The plan said, before anything was logged: *"Monday 7:15pm is the
highest-risk hour of your week for both the drink and the vape, and it
recurs 52 times a year."* Entry 1 is a Monday, after futsal, in the
bathroom. It happened on the first Monday after that sentence was
written.

The state is specific and it is not willpower: you are **hungry,
physically tired, and walking into the back half of a bedtime** — three
hard hours compressed into one, with a game in front of them.

> **When I walk in from futsal and the boys are still up, I take over
> the story and read it — sitting down, in the room, before I eat.**

Why this and not something else:

- **It is performed.** You read a book. There is a thing to do with
  your hands and your voice.
- **It is in the room.** The bathroom was the destination; this
  removes the walk rather than resisting it.
- **It serves a second goal.** *"Properly present at bedtime"* is one
  of your seven, and Monday was the night the plan had already written
  off. It is not written off.
- **Food comes after, deliberately.** The mackerel is no-cook for
  exactly this reason — it waits. Twenty minutes of story then dinner
  at 19:50 is a worse dinner time and a better Monday.

**The back-up branch**, because the protocol says a plan that breaks
needs somewhere to go: *When the story is done and I still want it, I
make a drink slowly and sit down with it, in the kitchen, with the
light on.* The app's own stand-in vocabulary calls that `water` — *"a
drink made slowly, away from the desk."*

## Two — the quiet house, any night at 20:30

The drink's window, not the vape's. Entry 1 did not test this one; the
alcohol was zero, which means this if-then has held once without being
written down.

> **When the second one is down and I come back downstairs, I put the
> kettle on and take the first ten minutes outside — phone staying in
> the other room where it already is.**

Why:

- **The cue is a physical event**, not a feeling. Coming down the
  stairs. You cannot be unsure whether it happened.
- **Two performed actions, not one.** The kettle occupies the hands in
  the kitchen, which is the room where the decision gets made; outside
  changes the room entirely.
- **It is `detachment-window` starting.** Graded B, called *"the off
  switch"*, sixty minutes — and 20:30–22:00 is the ninety blank
  minutes the plan calls its hardest unsolved problem. This is the
  first ten of them having a shape.

**Back-up branch:** *When I have come down and the house is quiet and I
want one, I message someone — anyone — and say what I'm doing tonight.*
That is `message`: *"message someone who knows."* It is also the thing
your own profile says works: plans with other people in them survive a
bad week.

## Three — the wedding, Saturday 14 November

Event one of your two for November. It is in five days and it is the
only one of these three with a date on it.

> **When I get to the wedding, I put the vape in the glovebox before I
> go in, and the first drink I order is water — one in each hand for
> the first hour.**

Why this shape:

- **The vape decision is made in the car park**, not in a function
  room at eleven. This is the hole in your event allowance: *every
  drinking night you allow is also a vaping night*, and the only
  reliable version of that plan is physical distance.
- **"One in each hand"** is performed, visible and socially legible.
  Nobody asks a man holding a drink whether he wants a drink.
- **The event is a night, not a weekend.** Written here so Sunday
  lunch is already decided.

**Back-up branch:** *When somebody puts a drink in my hand I did not
ask for, I hold it, thank them, and put it down on the nearest flat
surface.* Performed, not withheld, and it skips the conversation
entirely.

## How to actually use these

The protocol is **fifteen minutes, once a week, Sundays** — it is a
practice, not a one-off. `if-then-plan` (also grade A) is the same
fifteen minutes pointed at the work week.

- **Say them out loud once.** The mechanism is recall in the situation,
  which means the sentence has to be retrievable, not filed.
- **Rewrite them on Sunday**, with the half hour that already exists
  for the food. The situations will change; entry 1 already moved one.
- **A broken plan is information, not a failure.** Log what happened
  and which branch it got to. Four entries is where the pattern read
  switches on.

## What these cannot do

- **An if-then is for a habit you can change on your own.** The
  protocol's own safety line: *"not treatment, and not a plan for
  physical dependence. If going without brings on shakes, sweating,
  nausea or bad anxiety, see a doctor before you change anything."*
- **The nicotine half still has no protocol in this library.** All 323
  searched. The strongest evidence in cessation is combination
  pharmacotherapy plus behavioural support, which roughly doubles to
  triples quit rates against willpower alone. That is a GP
  conversation and these three sentences are not a substitute for it.
- **Number one is the only one with evidence behind it**, and that
  evidence is a single entry. Two and three are reasoned from the plan,
  not observed.
