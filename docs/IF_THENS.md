# The three if-thens

`trigger-if-then` — **evidence level A**, the highest grade in this
app. Gollwitzer and Sheeran. *"Around ninety-four studies pooled to a
medium-to-large effect, working hardest on the intentions people keep
failing to act on, because the decision is already made before the
moment arrives."*

Of 323 protocols, 15 are grade A. This is one of them, and it is the
one aimed squarely at what you are doing.

---

## The two rules that rule out most of what people write

Both are from the protocol's own text, and both matter more than the
wording:

**1. Name a real situation, not a vague day.** *"The fourth round at
the same table, the phone at 11pm."* Not "when I'm stressed." A
situation you would recognise from the outside.

**2. The "then" must be something you DO, not something you withhold.**
*"Plans written as 'I will not' leave you nothing to do with the
moment."* This is why "I won't vape tonight" is not an if-then. It is
an intention, which is the thing you already had.

And the reason the frame matters at all, in the protocol's words:
*"the claim that self-control is a fuel tank that empties has failed
large multi-lab replication, so any plan whose active ingredient is
wanting it more is not a plan."*

---

## One — Monday, walking in from futsal

**This is the one that is now evidenced rather than predicted.**

The plan said, before anything was logged: *"Monday 7:15pm is the
highest-risk hour of your week for both the drink and the vape, and it
recurs 52 times a year."* Entry 1 is a Monday, after futsal, in the
bathroom. It happened on the first Monday after that sentence was
written.

The state is specific and it is not willpower: you are **hungry,
physically tired, and walking into the back half of a bedtime** — three
hard hours compressed into one, with a game in front of them.

> **When I walk in from futsal and the boys are still up, I take over
> the story and read it — sitting down, in the room, before I eat.**

Why this and not something else:

- **It is performed.** You read a book. There is a thing to do with
  your hands and your voice.
- **It is in the room.** The bathroom was the destination; this
  removes the walk rather than resisting it.
- **It serves a second goal.** *"Properly present at bedtime"* is one
  of your seven, and Monday was the night the plan had already written
  off. It is not written off.
- **Food comes after, deliberately.** The mackerel is no-cook for
  exactly this reason — it waits. Twenty minutes of story then dinner
  at 19:50 is a worse dinner time and a better Monday.

**The back-up branch**, because the protocol says a plan that breaks
needs somewhere to go: *When the story is done and I still want it, I
make a drink slowly and sit down with it, in the kitchen, with the
light on.* The app's own stand-in vocabulary calls that `water` — *"a
drink made slowly, away from the desk."*

## Two — walking in the door at 16:40, with the boys

**Rewritten twice.** First version: coming downstairs at 20:30, into
the quiet house. Second: the kitchen at 18:00, getting dinner ready.
Both wrong, and wrong the same way.

> *"I drink probably closer to straight after work which should be the
> real trigger point."*

Work ends 16:30. The office is two minutes from home, the daycare is a
two-minute drive, and the drink comes **after, once everybody is
home** — so you finish work and are in the house with both boys
**about ten minutes later.**

### The real problem, which is not the drink

**You have no transition.** Most people have a commute doing this job
badly. You have six hundred seconds.

So the drink is not competing with a quiet evening, or with dinner, or
with willpower. **It is the only decompression in your day, arriving at
the only moment that has none.** That changes what the if-then has to
be: not a different thing to hold, but the transition that does not
exist.

### The finding this produced, which is better than anything I reasoned to

**Both behaviours are cued on the same thing: crossing the threshold
into the house in a depleted state.**

| arrival | time | behaviour |
| --- | --- | --- |
| home from work, with the boys | **~16:40** | **the drink** |
| home from futsal | **~19:15** | **the vape** |

It is one cue at two times. That is why if-then one works on the same
shape as this one, and it is the only structural thing in this whole
plan that came from the data rather than from me guessing.

> **When I pull up after pickup, I don't go in — I take the boys
> straight round the block or into the garden for fifteen minutes
> before anyone opens the front door.**

Fifteen minutes is the entire ask, and it is the only gap in the day
between finishing work and being on duty at home.

Why this and not a substitute drink:

- **It removes the moment rather than resisting it.** The threshold is
  the cue. Don't cross it yet. There is nothing to refuse because you
  are not in the room where the thing is.
- **It is performed, and it is with other people.** Two of your goals
  at once, and it is the category of plan your own profile says
  survives a bad week.
- **It is `the-weekday-shape`'s "one thing outside"** (graded B)
  happening at the moment it is most useful rather than as a box.
- **It costs fifteen minutes and dinner moves to 18:15.** That is the
  whole price.

**Back-up branch:** *When we come in and I still want one, I pour a
soda water with lime into a proper glass and put it by my plate before
I sit down.* That was the previous version of this if-then, and it is
better as a second line than a first.

### What this breaks, and it is the piece I said had survived

**The 17:30 "decide tonight" slot is nearly an hour too late.** I
wrote yesterday that it was "the only part of the plan aimed at the
window the data names." It is not. A 16:40 drink is poured fifty
minutes before the decision gets made.

And the car is not the place for it either: two minutes to daycare and
two minutes home is not a decompression, it is a manoeuvre. **The
decision has to happen at your desk before you stand up.**

Everything in the evening now moves earlier:

| | was | now |
| --- | --- | --- |
| decide tonight | 17:30 | **16:25, at your desk before you leave** |
| the drink's cue | — | **~16:40, at the door** |
| the new walk | — | **16:40, before going in** |
| the post-meal walk | 18:30 | 18:30, unchanged |
| kitchen closed | 19:30 | 19:30, and it is about food only |

The 18:30 walk stays where it is. `post-meal-walk` is graded B
*specifically* for the glucose spike after the biggest meal, and moving
it would keep the habit and throw away the reason. So there are two
walks now, which is honestly a lot — **if one has to go, keep the
16:40 one.** It is aimed at a behaviour; the other is aimed at a
metabolite.

## Three — the wedding, Saturday 14 November

Event one of your two for November. It is in five days and it is the
only one of these three with a date on it.

> **When I get to the wedding, I put the vape in the glovebox before I
> go in, and the first drink I order is water — one in each hand for
> the first hour.**

Why this shape:

- **The vape decision is made in the car park**, not in a function
  room at eleven. This is the hole in your event allowance: *every
  drinking night you allow is also a vaping night*, and the only
  reliable version of that plan is physical distance.
- **"One in each hand"** is performed, visible and socially legible.
  Nobody asks a man holding a drink whether he wants a drink.
- **The event is a night, not a weekend.** Written here so Sunday
  lunch is already decided.

**Back-up branch:** *When somebody puts a drink in my hand I did not
ask for, I hold it, thank them, and put it down on the nearest flat
surface.* Performed, not withheld, and it skips the conversation
entirely.

## How to actually use these

The protocol is **fifteen minutes, once a week, Sundays** — it is a
practice, not a one-off. `if-then-plan` (also grade A) is the same
fifteen minutes pointed at the work week.

- **Say them out loud once.** The mechanism is recall in the situation,
  which means the sentence has to be retrievable, not filed.
- **Rewrite them on Sunday**, with the half hour that already exists
  for the food. The situations will change; entry 1 already moved one.
- **A broken plan is information, not a failure.** Log what happened
  and which branch it got to. Four entries is where the pattern read
  switches on.

## What these cannot do

- **An if-then is for a habit you can change on your own.** The
  protocol's own safety line: *"not treatment, and not a plan for
  physical dependence. If going without brings on shakes, sweating,
  nausea or bad anxiety, see a doctor before you change anything."*
- **The nicotine half still has no protocol in this library.** All 323
  searched. The strongest evidence in cessation is combination
  pharmacotherapy plus behavioural support, which roughly doubles to
  triples quit rates against willpower alone. That is a GP
  conversation and these three sentences are not a substitute for it.
- **One and two now rest on five entries**, which is just over the
  threshold `patterns.ts` uses (`MIN_EVENTS_FOR_PATTERN` is 4) — but
  five entries from **one** run of consecutive nights is not five
  independent observations. Treat ~16:40 as the best number available,
  not a measured habit.
- **I have had this window wrong three times, always in the same
  direction.** 20:30, then 18:00, now ~16:40. Each correction came from
  you rather than from anything the app or I measured, and each moved
  it earlier. If there is a fourth correction it will probably move
  earlier again, so the thing to watch is whether 16:40 is really the
  first one or just the first one that gets noticed.
- **Those five nights were "worse than usual — a bad week."** So
  nothing here should be read as a description of a normal week. What
  a normal week looks like is still unknown, and a fortnight of
  entries is the only thing that will say.
- **`weekdayShape` has nothing to report.** Five consecutive days,
  nearly evenly, is not a concentration — so there is no Thursday
  pattern, no weekend pattern, and the Monday prediction holding once
  does not make Mondays a pattern either.
- **Three is still reasoned, not observed.** No event has been logged
  at a wedding.
