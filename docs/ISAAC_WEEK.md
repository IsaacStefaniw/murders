## 1. The mechanism, which is one thing and not seven

You do not have seven problems. You have **one chain**, and five of
your goals are hanging off it.

You told me the vaping trigger is *"drinking or stressed"*, and then
you told me one of the three stresses is **the kids at the hard end of
the day.** That is 17:30 to 19:30, every weekday, entirely
predictable.

> **Hard handover (17:30–19:30) → stress → vape. Boys down (19:30) →
> the house goes quiet → the drink. Then the kitchen, then the phone,
> then a late bed, then a worse tomorrow — which makes the next
> handover harder.**

Two consequences, and they reorganise the whole plan:

1. **19:30–22:00 is where the damage lands, not where it starts.** A
   plan that only defends the quiet evening is intervening one step
   too late, every day. The leverage is at **19:00–19:30 — the
   handover itself.**
2. **It is a loop, so any single cut weakens the whole thing.** Sleep
   is the cheapest place to cut it: short sleep raises impulsivity and
   reward-seeking, which makes both the vape and the drink harder to
   refuse the following evening. This is why the answer to §2 is what
   it is.

## 2. You need neither 5am nor a compromise

You said the gym *"has to be at lunch or early mornings"*, and offered
05:30. Then you mentioned two things that change the answer: the
office is **two minutes from the house**, and your partner does
**Tuesday and Friday** pickups.

So you have four good slots and none of them is 5am:

| slot | length | your own measured affinity |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Lunch, any weekday** | 30 min | midday **0.30** |
| **Tue & Fri, 17:00 at home** | 30 min | evening **0.68 — your best** |
| 05:30 morning | 50 min | morning **0.22 — your worst** |

Those numbers are from your own profile in this repo, written from
your answers months before this conversation. The note beside them
reads: *"Believes in the 06:30 gym session. Does it about a fifth of
the time, and far more often after work than before it."*

**So: two lunch sessions and two Tuesday/Friday evening sessions.**
Four sessions, zero sleep cost, and the two evening ones land on your
single best slot of the week.

**Why not 05:30, said properly.** Bed 22:00, wake 05:30, and
*"sometimes woken in the night"* by two under-fives gives you a
6½-hour night that is already being raided. Per §1, sleep is the rung
the whole loop rests on — a quit attempt funded by a sleep deficit is
a quit attempt with the brakes off. You would be borrowing from goal
one to pay for goal three, and from goal six as well, since *"not
short-tempered with them"* is mostly a sleep and alcohol outcome.

**Where 05:30 earns its place:** as the make-up slot, not the plan. A
lunch that gets eaten by a meeting moves to 05:30 the next morning
rather than vanishing. A slot you use ten times a year on purpose is
worth having. A slot you plan for four times a week and use once is
the thing that taught you to ignore the app.

## 3. The four-week block

Generated by `buildProgramme` — `goal: 'fatloss'`,
`experience: 'consistent'`, `level: 'established'`, 4 days, 30-minute
sessions, full equipment. **Four changes I made to its output, each
with a reason**, because the generator has two outright bugs and two
bad calls at this session length:

- **It named no sessions** — `undefined` for every one. A bug.
- **It put Romanian deadlifts on the lower day underneath squats and
  deadlifts.** Three hip hinges in one session is not a programme, and
  at 30 minutes it is not even possible.
- **The accessory block was identical on all four days.**
- **It budgeted for more than 30 minutes.** I cut each session to a
  main lift and one or two partners, and kept the main lift's rest
  intact — the rest is what makes it a strength session rather than
  circuit training.

Every session: **5 minutes to warm up, 25 to work.** Supersets marked
**A1/A2** — alternate them, which is how four exercises fit in 25
minutes without cutting rest on what matters.

### Monday — lunch, Lower A

| lift | sets × reps | effort | rest |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Squat | 3 × 6–10 | RPE 7 | 120s |
| A1 Romanian deadlift | 3 × 8–10 | RPE 7 | — |
| A2 Leg extension | 3 × 10–12 | RPE 7 | 75s |

### Tuesday — 17:00 at home, Upper A

| lift | sets × reps | effort | rest |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Bench press | 3 × 6–10 | RPE 7 | 120s |
| A1 Barbell row | 3 × 6–10 | RPE 7 | — |

### How the four weeks move

This is the part that makes it a block rather than a list, and it is
straight from the generator:

- **Weeks 1–2 — build.** Exactly as above, both weeks.
- **Week 3 — progress.** One more set on the main lift, rep range
  tightens: squat, bench, deadlift and incline go to **4 × 6–8**,
  second lift to **3 × 6–8**. Same loads, more of them.
- **Week 4 — deload.** **2 × 8 at RPE 6**, accessories cut to one.
  Not a week off — a week that makes week 5 heavier.

**RPE 7 means you could have done three more reps.** Everything is
effort-anchored because the system has no lift numbers for you.

**You said you know your working weights — send me the four** (squat,
bench, deadlift, overhead press) and I will re-cut this block
prescribed in kilos, with the week-3 progression and the deload
calculated off them. That is what auto-regulation needs and it needs
nothing else.

### Where the cardio went

The generator wanted a 5 × 30s interval finisher on all four days. At
30 minutes, with a fat-loss goal, in a fortnight when you are also
quitting alcohol, on broken sleep, that is too much and it is the
first thing that would make you skip a session.

It is not needed. The **18:30 walk in §7** is daily, it is a graded
protocol, and it brings the boys. Add intervals in block two if you
want them.

## 4. The numbers

From `buildNutritionPlan`, aim `weight` (fat loss keeps protein
**high** — that is exactly what protects the muscle), 90 kg, trouble
`evenings`:

| | |
| --- | --- |
| **protein** | **162–198 g/day** — about **55 g across 3 meals** |
| **fibre** | **30 g/day** |
| **the plate** | *"Protein and vegetables first, then decide if you still want the rest — order does quiet work."* |

**An honest caveat on the protein figure**, which this project's own
research notes insist on: the 1.6 g/kg number everyone quotes comes
from a break point at **p = 0.079, CI 1.03–2.20** (Morton et al.
2018). It is a **range, not a threshold.** The repo's own ladder file
says *"give a range, never a point"*, and a card claiming a precise
per-meal number was demoted from B to C in September for this reason.

**So aim at 165–180 g.** Landing on 150 is not a failure; 200 buys
nothing. Three meals with a solid palm-and-a-half of protein each gets
you there without counting a thing.

### The lever ladder

Reordered by the app because you said evenings. All four are live at
once, because each is a single decision rather than a daily effort:

1. **Kitchen closes** — `kitchen-closed`. For you, **19:30** — the
   moment the boys go down, which is also the moment the chain in §1
   fires. *"The window itself is not magic — it works because a closed
   kitchen is the easy way to eat less at night."*
2. **10-minute walk after the biggest meal** — `post-meal-walk`,
   graded **B** (Buffey et al., *Sports Med* 2022, 7 randomised
   crossover trials). The caveat the repo records and I will not hide:
   **all acute, all single-day, no long-run outcome measured.** Do it
   anyway — it costs ten minutes and it is doing four jobs at once.
3. **Water is the default drink** — `default-drink-water`. Quietly
   important now that alcohol is going: *"a sweet one becomes a choice
   rather than the thing in your hand."*
4. **Build toward 30 g fibre** — `fibre-30`. *"The best-supported
   dietary number there is."* Beans, lentils, oats, fruit, nuts, skins.

No diet, no logging, no calorie counting. Four decisions and a protein
floor.

## 5. Alcohol

Your plan: **stop, except 2–3 named events a month**, with Saturday's
wedding as the first.

**Keep it. It is a better mechanism than it looks.** The graded
protocol here is `drink-free-days` — *"name the drink-free days before
the week starts"* — and its stated reason is behavioural: *"a rule
decided in advance removes a nightly decision that tends to resolve
one way."* You have inverted it: instead of naming the dry days you
are naming the **wet** ones. Same mechanism, pointed the other way,
and strictly stronger — because now the default is no.

Three conditions, or it leaks:

1. **Named in advance and written down, or it is not an event.** "A
   big event" decided at 6pm on a Friday is just a Friday. Put them on
   a calendar at the start of the month. Saturday is event one of
   November.
2. **An event is a night, not a weekend.** The failure mode is the
   Saturday wedding becoming Sunday lunch.
3. **Two, not three, in the first month.** You are removing a daily
   habit and a weekend habit at once. Less rope at the start.

### The hole in the allowance

You said the vaping happens *"only when drinking or stressed."*

> **Every drinking event you allow is also a vaping event. The
> allowance has a hole in it and the hole is nicotine.**

So **do not quit the vape first.** Quit the drink first, because on
your own account the vape is downstream of it — and of the handover in
§1. Remove both triggers and you are quitting a much smaller habit a
fortnight later.

And each of your 2–3 events needs a **vape plan** decided before you
leave the house, not at 11pm in a function room. Leave it at home.
That is the entire plan and it beats any rule you will make while
holding one.

### Said once, because it matters

The protocol's own safety text: *"If cutting down feels hard, if you
drink every day, or if stopping brings shakes, sweating, a racing
heart or bad anxiety, talk to a doctor before changing anything —
stopping abruptly is genuinely unsafe for some people, and effective
help exists and works."*

You described **midweek at home plus heavy weekends.** That is enough
to be worth a GP conversation rather than a planning document, and I
cannot tell from here which side of the line you are on.

## 6. Vaping, and an honest gap

**There is no vaping protocol in this repo.** I searched all 323: zero
on nicotine, zero on vaping, nothing on smoking beyond a
`smokingStatus` question used for a health marker. So nothing in this
section carries a grade from the library, and I am not going to borrow
one from the alcohol cards to make it look sturdier than it is.

What the graded urge machinery does give you, and it transfers because
an urge is an urge:

- **`urge-tonight-plan`** — graded **B**, the best-evidenced thing in
  the urge family. *"Tonight, in your own words."* Decide what tonight
  looks like **before** it starts. Given §1, do this at **19:00**, as
  the boys go down — the moment the chain fires.
- **`urge-log`** — graded **B**. One line per urge: what happened,
  where you were, what you did, *written before any judgement about
  it.* Its own card says why: *"the fastest way to find that the real
  trigger is six o'clock and an empty house rather than stress, which
  is what nearly everyone assumes."* You have **named** your triggers.
  The log is how you find out whether you are right.
- **`urge-stand-in`** — graded C. A replacement that fits the trigger,
  chosen in advance. Hands need something. C is honest; the evidence
  is thin.
- **`urge-next-hour`** — graded C. After a slip, the next hour. Not
  the next day, not next Monday.

**One thing I will say that is not in the library:** the strongest
evidence in nicotine cessation is not behavioural at all — it is
combination pharmacotherapy plus behavioural support, which roughly
doubles to triples quit rates against willpower alone. That is a GP or
pharmacist conversation, it is cheap, and skipping it means choosing
the hard version for no reason. Signposting, not prescribing.

**Sequencing: alcohol from this week. Vape from week 3**, once both
triggers are handled. Not both at once.

## 9. Do these in this order

Stacking seven changes in one week is how all seven fail. Four
fortnights:

**Weeks 1–2 — the hinge and the drink.**
Alcohol stops except named events. 19:00 phone out and *tonight in
your own words*. 19:30 kitchen closed. The 18:30 walk. The 21:45 log.
Training carries on as it is. **Do not start a deficit this
fortnight** — removing midweek and weekend drinking is already a large
calorie reduction and may do most of the fat loss by itself. Nothing
else is new.

**Weeks 3–4 — nutrition, the vape, and the block properly.**
Protein to 165–180 g. Fibre toward 30. Water as default. The vape
stops, now both triggers are handled. Start the four-week block and
**write down your loads.**

**Weeks 5–8 — block two, and only now a deficit.**
Prescribed in kilos from your own numbers. If the scale hasn't moved
and you want it to, a modest deficit goes here — on top of a settled
week, not underneath an unsettled one.

**Week 9 — read the log.**
Twelve weeks of one-line entries will tell you something you do not
currently know about when the urges actually land. Its own card says
the trigger is usually not what people assume. You have assumed
drinking, work, the kids and money. Find out which it really is.

## 10. What this cannot tell you

- **The protein number is a range**, and the per-meal figure is weaker
  than it looks. p = 0.079.
- **The post-meal walk evidence is entirely acute** — seven crossover
  trials, one day each, no long-run outcome.
- **There is no nicotine evidence in this library at all.** §6 is the
  honest version rather than a dressed-up one.
- **§7's Friday review has no evidence behind it.** It is a
  suggestion.
- **Nothing here is medical advice**, and two items on your list —
  daily-plus-weekend drinking, and nicotine dependence — have real
  medical help that works better than planning does. Use it.
- **A plan is not an outcome.** This app's simulator has no model of
  anybody quitting anything, which is exactly why it never predicted
  you would stop using it.
