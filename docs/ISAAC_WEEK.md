## 1. The mechanism, which is one thing and not seven

You do not have seven problems. You have **one chain**, and five of
your goals are hanging off it.

You told me the vaping trigger is *"drinking or stressed"*, and that
one of the three stresses is **the kids at the hard end of the day.**
The boys are asleep at **20:00–20:30**, so that hard end runs
**17:30 to 20:30 — three hours, every weekday, entirely
predictable.**

> **Three hard hours (17:30–20:30) → stress → vape. Boys asleep at
> 20:30 → the house goes quiet → the drink. Then the kitchen, then the
> phone, then a late bed, then a worse tomorrow — which makes the next
> three hours harder.**

Three consequences, and they reorganise the plan:

1. **The quiet window is 20:30–22:00. Ninety minutes, not two and a
   half hours.** That is better news than it sounds: it is a small,
   fixed, nameable block, and five of them is the whole weekly problem
   — about seven and a half hours a week to put a job to.
2. **But the hard stretch is three hours, longer than I first
   modelled.** So the intervention cannot sit at the end of it. A
   decision made at 20:30, by a man who has just done bath, books and
   two refusals to go to sleep, is a decision made by the tired
   version of you.
   **Decide at 17:30, before the hard part starts, not after it.**
3. **It is a loop, so any single cut weakens the whole thing.** Sleep
   is the cheapest place to cut it: short sleep raises impulsivity and
   reward-seeking, which makes both the vape and the drink harder to
   refuse the following evening. That is why the answer to §2 is what
   it is.

### Monday night futsal changes the week, mostly for the better

You play **every Monday at 6:15pm**, and you had not mentioned it, so
the first version of this plan put squats at Monday lunch and expected
you at bedtime on Monday evening. Both were wrong.

**Futsal is the most valuable thing in your week and I nearly
programmed over it.** Your own profile says it twice: *"plans with
other people in them survive a bad week; plans with only themselves in
them do not."* A fixed weekly commitment with other people in it is
the single most durable item you have — more durable than anything in
this document. It also does the conditioning the block deliberately
left out, which is why there are no interval finishers anywhere.

So the week reorganises around it:

- **Monday is a leg day already.** Repeated sprinting, decelerating and
  changing direction is a large lower-body load. Squats at Monday lunch
  then futsal at 6:15 is stacking two hard leg sessions into nine
  hours. **Monday lunch becomes upper body**, which does not compete.
- **Lower body moves to Wednesday and Friday** — 48 hours after futsal
  and 72 hours before the next one. That cycles properly.
- **Tuesday stays upper**, because sore legs do not matter for an
  overhead press.
- **Thursday lifts nothing.** Walk only.

**And Monday bedtime is half-saved, not lost.** The game ends about
7:15pm and the boys are not asleep until 20:00–20:30, so you are home
for the back half — books and lights-out, if not bath. That is a
better Monday than the first draft of this plan assumed, when I had
them down at 19:00 and wrote Monday off entirely.

What you miss on a Monday is dinner with them and the walk. Worth
knowing, not worth fixing: a weekly game with other people in it will
outlast any evening habit in this document.

### The Monday-night risk nobody flags

Post-game, 7:15pm, hungry, tired, with the lads. Monday is also one of
your *"midweek at home"* drinking nights.

> **Monday 7:15pm is the highest-risk hour of your week for both the
> drink and the vape, and it recurs 52 times a year.**

### Why the first version was too light, which you caught

You said: *"those bench press kilos are way too light, I could do 15
reps of 90 consistently."* You are right, and there are two separate
errors stacked on each other.

**One — your stated 130 looked like an understatement.** Fifteen reps
at 90 kg implies an e1RM of **135 (Epley) to 147 (Brzycki)**, so I
called it 138 on the principle that a set you actually did beats a
remembered max.

**That principle is right and I applied it badly.** Two more sets later
the figure settled at ~132 and **your 130 was the best number anybody
had.** The 138 below is left as written because the rest of this
section is about a real bug and it is the number that bug was computed
against — see *Your max* further down for where it landed.

**Two — and this one is the generator's.** Its scheme for this goal:

| week | sets × reps | %e1RM |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | 3 × 6–10 | 0.70 |
| 2 | 3 × 6–10 | 0.725 |
| 3 | 4 × 6–8 | 0.75 |
| 4 | 2 × 8 | 0.62 |

**0.70 of a one-rep max is roughly a twelve-rep weight, prescribed
against a six-to-ten rep range.** That is coherent only at the very top
of the range: at 10 reps you finish about 2 short, which is right; at 6
reps you finish 6 short, which is a warm-up. One load for a four-rep
window, pegged at the easy end.

For you it was worse, because the percentage was applied to an
understated max. **90 kg is 65% of a 138 e1RM — about your fifteen-rep
weight — prescribed for sets of 6–10.** Stopping five to nine reps
short, four times a week, in a deficit, is how you lose muscle while
training.

Same class of mistake I made three times today in the other direction:
a number never checked against the thing it describes. It is also
precisely what `trainingProgramme`'s auto-regulation exists to catch
and cannot, because nothing in the app ever asks how the set felt.

### The progression rule — three lines, and they are yours

1. Ramp to one top set: **8, then 5, then 5, then 3.**
2. When the **triple** goes up with a rep still in the tank, the top

3. **Every fourth week the top two sets come off.** Nothing else
   changes.

rung lags one session and then catches up. The app says *the top set*

## 4. The numbers

From `buildNutritionPlan`, aim `weight` (fat loss keeps protein
**high** — that is exactly what protects the muscle), 90 kg, trouble
`evenings`:

| | |
| --- | --- |
| **protein** | **162–198 g/day** — about **55 g across 3 meals** |
| **fibre** | **30 g/day** |
| **the plate** | *"Protein and vegetables first, then decide if you still want the rest — order does quiet work."* |

**An honest caveat on the protein figure**, which this project's own
research notes insist on: the 1.6 g/kg number everyone quotes comes
from a break point at **p = 0.079, CI 1.03–2.20** (Morton et al.
2018). It is a **range, not a threshold.** The repo's own ladder file
says *"give a range, never a point"*, and a card claiming a precise
per-meal number was demoted from B to C in September for this reason.

**So aim at 165–180 g.** Landing on 150 is not a failure; 200 buys
nothing. Three meals with a solid palm-and-a-half of protein each gets
you there without counting a thing.

### The lever ladder

Reordered by the app because you said evenings. All four are live at
once, because each is a single decision rather than a daily effort:

1. **Kitchen closes** — `kitchen-closed`. For you, **19:30** — the
   moment the boys go down, which is also the moment the chain in §1
   fires. *"The window itself is not magic — it works because a closed
   kitchen is the easy way to eat less at night."*
2. **10-minute walk after the biggest meal** — `post-meal-walk`,
   graded **B** (Buffey et al., *Sports Med* 2022, 7 randomised
   crossover trials). The caveat the repo records and I will not hide:
   **all acute, all single-day, no long-run outcome measured.** Do it
   anyway — it costs ten minutes and it is doing four jobs at once.
3. **Water is the default drink** — `default-drink-water`. Quietly
   important now that alcohol is going: *"a sweet one becomes a choice
   rather than the thing in your hand."*
4. **Build toward 30 g fibre** — `fibre-30`. *"The best-supported
   dietary number there is."* Beans, lentils, oats, fruit, nuts, skins.

No diet, no logging, no calorie counting. Four decisions and a protein
floor.

### Monday, because a game needs fuelling and a deficit does not change that

Nothing in this library covers pre-training nutrition. All 323
protocols searched: **no peri-workout protocol exists.** So this is
standard sports nutrition and carries no grade from the repo.

Futsal is repeated high-intensity sprinting, which runs on glycogen.
Under-eating before it does not accelerate fat loss, it just makes you
worse at futsal.

| when | what | why |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **16:45** | 60–80 g carbs, ~20 g protein, **low fat, low fibre** — white rice, a bagel, bread with honey, plus a shake or some lean chicken | 90 minutes is enough to empty the stomach and still have the fuel |
| **17:45** | 500 ml water, pinch of salt | indoor court, you sweat more than you notice |
| **19:30** | the biggest protein hit of the day, **50–60 g** | it is also the only way a Monday reaches 165 g |

**Avoid in that 90-minute window:** chilli, fish sauce, a big raw
salad, anything fatty. All fine at lunch. Not fine 90 minutes before
sprinting.

**Monday's kitchen does not close at 19:30.** It cannot. Eat the
post-game meal, then close it. The rule is five nights, not seven, and
pretending otherwise is how a rule gets abandoned.

## 5. Alcohol

Your plan: **stop, except 2–3 named events a month**, with Saturday's
wedding as the first.

**Keep it. It is a better mechanism than it looks.** The graded
protocol here is `drink-free-days` — *"name the drink-free days before
the week starts"* — and its stated reason is behavioural: *"a rule
decided in advance removes a nightly decision that tends to resolve
one way."* You have inverted it: instead of naming the dry days you
are naming the **wet** ones. Same mechanism, pointed the other way,
and strictly stronger — because now the default is no.

Three conditions, or it leaks:

1. **Named in advance and written down, or it is not an event.** "A
   big event" decided at 6pm on a Friday is just a Friday. Put them on
   a calendar at the start of the month. Saturday is event one of
   November.
2. **An event is a night, not a weekend.** The failure mode is the
   Saturday wedding becoming Sunday lunch.
3. **Two, not three, in the first month.** You are removing a daily
   habit and a weekend habit at once. Less rope at the start.

### The hole in the allowance

You said the vaping happens *"only when drinking or stressed."*

> **Every drinking event you allow is also a vaping event. The
> allowance has a hole in it and the hole is nicotine.**

So **do not quit the vape first.** Quit the drink first, because on
your own account the vape is downstream of it — and of the handover in
§1. Remove both triggers and you are quitting a much smaller habit a
fortnight later.

And each of your 2–3 events needs a **vape plan** decided before you
leave the house, not at 11pm in a function room. Leave it at home.
That is the entire plan and it beats any rule you will make while
holding one.

### Said once, because it matters

You described **midweek at home plus heavy weekends.** That is enough

## 6. Vaping, and an honest gap

**There is no vaping protocol in this repo.** I searched all 323: zero
on nicotine, zero on vaping, nothing on smoking beyond a
`smokingStatus` question used for a health marker. So nothing in this
section carries a grade from the library, and I am not going to borrow
one from the alcohol cards to make it look sturdier than it is.

What the graded urge machinery does give you, and it transfers because
an urge is an urge:

- **`urge-tonight-plan`** — graded **B**, the best-evidenced thing in
  the urge family. *"Tonight, in your own words."* Decide what tonight
  looks like **before** it starts. Given §1, do this at **19:00**, as
  the boys go down — the moment the chain fires.
- **`urge-log`** — graded **B**. One line per urge: what happened,
  where you were, what you did, *written before any judgement about
  it.* Its own card says why: *"the fastest way to find that the real
  trigger is six o'clock and an empty house rather than stress, which
  is what nearly everyone assumes."* You have **named** your triggers.
  The log is how you find out whether you are right.
- **`urge-stand-in`** — graded C. A replacement that fits the trigger,
  chosen in advance. Hands need something. C is honest; the evidence
  is thin.
- **`urge-next-hour`** — graded C. After a slip, the next hour. Not
  the next day, not next Monday.

**One thing I will say that is not in the library:** the strongest
evidence in nicotine cessation is not behavioural at all — it is
combination pharmacotherapy plus behavioural support, which roughly
doubles to triples quit rates against willpower alone. That is a GP or
pharmacist conversation, it is cheap, and skipping it means choosing
the hard version for no reason. Signposting, not prescribing.

**Sequencing: alcohol from this week. Vape from week 3**, once both
triggers are handled. Not both at once.

### 17:30 is the hinge, and 20:30 is the window

This is the §1 correction and the most important structural line in the
plan. The decision and the danger are at **opposite ends** of the

**At 17:30, before the hard part — decide.**

## 9. Do these in this order

Stacking seven changes in one week is how all seven fail. Four
fortnights:

**Weeks 1–2 — the hinge and the drink.**
Alcohol stops except named events. 19:00 phone out and *tonight in
your own words*. 19:30 kitchen closed. The 18:30 walk. The 21:45 log.
Training carries on as it is. **Do not start a deficit this
fortnight** — removing midweek and weekend drinking is already a large
calorie reduction and may do most of the fat loss by itself. Nothing
else is new.

**Weeks 3–4 — nutrition, the vape, and the block properly.**
Protein to 165–180 g. Fibre toward 30. Water as default. The vape
stops, now both triggers are handled. Start the four-week block and
**write down your loads.**

**Weeks 5–8 — block two, and only now a deficit.**
Prescribed in kilos from your own numbers. If the scale hasn't moved
and you want it to, a modest deficit goes here — on top of a settled
week, not underneath an unsettled one.

**Week 9 — read the log.**
Twelve weeks of one-line entries will tell you something you do not
currently know about when the urges actually land. Its own card says
the trigger is usually not what people assume. You have assumed
drinking, work, the kids and money. Find out which it really is.

## 10. What this cannot tell you

- **The protein number is a range**, and the per-meal figure is weaker
  than it looks. p = 0.079.
- **The post-meal walk evidence is entirely acute** — seven crossover
  trials, one day each, no long-run outcome.
- **There is no nicotine evidence in this library at all.** §6 is the
  honest version rather than a dressed-up one.
- **§7's Friday review has no evidence behind it.** It is a
  suggestion.
- **The "typical × bench" column in §3 is rule-of-thumb**, from
  general strength-standard convention rather than a cited dataset.
  The ratio gap is large enough that it holds under any version of
  those conventions, but do not treat 1.30 as a measured constant.
- **Three of my four criticisms of the programme generator were
  wrong**, and §3 lists them. I read a field that does not exist and
  then put "two outright bugs" in a commit message.
- **Futsal was missing from the first three versions of this plan**,
  and it is the most durable thing in the week. A plan built from an
  interview only knows what the interview asked, and nothing in those
  thirty-nine questions asks about a weekly team sport.
- **The one real generator defect, you found, not me**: 0.70 of a
  one-rep max against a 6–10 rep range. I had reproduced its
  percentages faithfully, checked they matched its output, and never
  asked whether the percentage suited the rep range. Verifying the
  arithmetic is not verifying the prescription.
- **Three of the four ramps are estimates.** Squat, deadlift and
  overhead are your stated 110, 140 and 80 taken at the same 91% the
  bench works out to. Only the bench rests on sets I watched you
  describe. Your first session on each replaces the guess.
- **I had your bench max wrong three times and your own first answer
  was right.** 138, then 124–128, then ~132, against the 130 you said
  at the start — and each of my figures was presented as the measured
  one. The lesson this document kept drawing, that a logged set beats a
  remembered number, is true; it does not make an estimate computed
  from one logged set better than a lifter's own knowledge of himself.
- **The flat 3 × 6–10 block was my guess, not a protocol.** When the
  percentage bug was fixed I replaced it with a shape nobody had asked
  for, and it took you three messages — 120 × 3, twenty years, eighty
  kilo pulldowns for fifteen — to get the ramp you actually run into
  the programme. Fixing a bug is not the same as checking the thing you
  replaced it with.
- **Nothing here is medical advice**, and two items on your list —
  daily-plus-weekend drinking, and nicotine dependence — have real
  medical help that works better than planning does. Use it.
- **A plan is not an outcome.** This app's simulator has no model of
  anybody quitting anything, which is exactly why it never predicted
  you would stop using it.
