# IntentNorth — brief to agency

**From:** Chief Marketing Officer, IntentNorth
**To:** [Agency], creative and UX leads
**Date:** 4 September 2026
**Subject:** Review and redesign of intentnorth.app

---

## 1. Why you are reading this

We have a product we believe in and a website that does not sell it. I would
rather hand you that sentence than a deck arguing the opposite.

Two weeks ago we showed the site to a handful of people. Not one of them could
say what we do. We then ran five structured reviews with people who had never
heard of us. Four of the five would not have installed the app. The fifth could
not, because we are still in App Store review.

We have since rewritten the top of the page ourselves and it is materially
better. It is still not good enough, and the reason we are coming to you rather
than iterating again is that we have run out of the kind of problem we can
solve by writing more carefully. What is left is a structural and design
problem.

## 2. What the product actually is

IntentNorth is an iPhone app. You answer a set of questions once. It then
builds and maintains your week across nine areas of life — training, food,
sleep, mind (meditation, breathing, stillness, journaling), habits and urges,
focused work, money, your relationship, and family — and rebuilds that week
whenever yours changes.

Three things distinguish it, in the order we believe matters:

**It writes the plan; you do not.** Not suggestions, not a dashboard, not a
list of articles. Sessions, meals, practices and reviews placed at specific
hours around what you already have on. Four-week training plans written for the
goal you pick. You never build a programme.

**Every practice carries a rating for the evidence behind it.** 317 practices,
each graded A to E, with the source named and, where it matters, a plain-words
safety note. 268 carry one. We publish the weak ratings: 202 of the 317 are
rated Mixed or weaker and the app tells you which. We believe this is the most
defensible thing we own, and every cold reviewer independently agreed.

**It adapts, and it says why.** Slept badly, or short on time, and the session
shortens rather than cancels — it keeps the part that matters, drops the rest,
and shows its reasoning. Two missed Tuesdays and it stops scheduling Tuesdays.

Free forever, never paywalled: urge support, resets, and getting back after a
lapse. That is a commitment in the code, not a promotion.

## 3. Commercial position

- **Price:** AU$89.99/year, AU$14.99/month, AU$249 once. One price covers all
  nine areas. No tiers, nothing withheld, no upsell.
- **Status:** version 1.0 is in App Store review. iPhone only.
- **Competitive set:** Whoop, Rise, Fitbod, MacroFactor, Hevy, Sunsama,
  Headspace. Each does one of our nine well. None of them share what they know
  about you across domains.
- **Our commercial bet:** breadth at one price against a stack of
  single-purpose subscriptions.

**And the hardest thing in this brief:** breadth is also our biggest
credibility problem. The most product-literate reviewer we tested put it
plainly — "seven domains from one team means none of them are deep; this is the
single biggest credibility leak on the page." We need you to solve breadth as
an asset rather than let it read as thinness. We do not know how.

## 4. What the site must do

In priority order.

1. **Make a stranger able to say what we are, in one sentence, in ten seconds.**
2. **Make the breadth feel like depth**, not like a feature list.
3. **Make the evidence ratings the reason to trust us** — it is our strongest
   asset and it is currently a section rather than a spine.
4. **Convert to an App Store download** the day we are approved, and do
   something useful with the traffic until then.
5. **Not read as a calendar app.** It is the wrong category and the one people
   default to.

## 5. The audience, in their own words

Five reviewers, briefed only on who they were. Full transcripts in
`docs/AUDIENCE_REVIEW.md`; the useful summary is below.

| | Who | Verdict |
|---|---|---|
| Sam, 41 | Two young kids, full-time job, does not train | Would close the tab |
| Dev, 29 | Lifts four times a week, wears a Whoop, pays for three apps | "A content app with a good vocabulary" |
| Jo, 47 | Drinking most nights, wants to stop | "It looks like it's for a man who lifts" |
| Priya, 44 | Executive, ninety seconds, buys premium software without blinking | Could not write our pitch without apologising first |
| Plain-language | Assessing it as public health information | Reading age 17–19 against a target of 13 |

The single most damaging line on the page was a training example — a specific
barbell weight — which three of the five named as the exact moment they decided
the product was not for them. We have removed it. The underlying problem
remains: **our proof is drawn from the one area that is least universal.**

The consistent positives, which we would like preserved in anything you
propose:

- "202 of the 317 are Mixed or weaker" — four of five named this unprompted as
  the reason they started trusting us
- "No verdict. No streak to break." — the only line that stopped our
  habit-change reviewer scrolling
- "We never charge for someone's hardest moment"
- "Nothing leaves your phone" — no account, no analytics, no server

## 6. What we have already tried, so you do not repeat it

We are telling you our failures because they are the most useful thing we have.

**Abstraction.** "The personal operating system." "Moving time is easy;
changing the programme is valuable." Precise, and unreadable. Dead.

**Animation.** We built four diagrammatic animations of the mechanism. They
explained *how it works* to people who had not yet been told *what it is*. All
removed. If you propose motion, it should show the product, not the machinery.

**Vanity numbers.** "317 practices" answered a question nobody asked. Our own
founder misremembered it as 178 while briefing us, which settled the argument.
Numbers now live inside sentences.

**Defensive honesty.** We wrote three passages insisting our screenshots were
genuine. Four of five reviewers read them as guilt — "nobody says that unless
someone accused them." All deleted. This is the trap we fall into most: we are
so concerned with being truthful that we sound like we are on trial.

**Burying the offer.** Our price sat 88% of the way down the page.

## 7. Non-negotiables

These are not style preferences. They are constraints on the work.

1. **No fabricated product.** Every screen shown must be a photograph of the
   shipped app. No design comps presented as product, no invented data, no
   composite screens. We have a library of real captures with documented
   provenance.
2. **No invented proof.** No testimonials, user counts, results or reviews
   until they exist. We have none.
3. **Every number traceable.** All product figures are checked against the app
   source by an automated test suite that fails the build. If you propose a
   number, we will check it.
4. **Health content is educational.** Never diagnosis or personal advice.
   Anything touching alcohol, sleep or mental health carries a plain-language
   route to real help beside it, not in a footer.
5. **The hardest-moment support stays free**, and is never used as an upsell.
6. **Named educators are attribution, never endorsement.**
7. **Plain language.** We now fail our own build on a list of terms a general
   reader would have to look up.

## 8. What you have to work with

- **A library of real app captures** — nine areas, the week view, a session
  adapting to a bad night, the practice library with ratings, progress. Full
  provenance in `docs/APP_SCREENSHOTS.md`. We can commission specific captures
  with about a day's turnaround.
- **Three films**, 30s / 47s / 50s, landscape and vertical, all built from
  screen recordings of the real app.
- **A working design system** — warm ivory, eucalyptus, charcoal, restrained
  amber; editorial serif display against a clean sans. We are not attached to
  it. We are attached to not looking like quantified-self neon.
- **The site is Next.js/React on Cloudflare Workers**, with an automated
  guardrail suite covering product truth, plain language and rendered layout at
  eight widths. You can propose anything; we will keep the guardrails.

## 9. What we are asking you for

**Phase 1 — Review (2 weeks).** A cold, structured teardown. We want the
strategic call, not a list of tweaks. Specifically:

- Who should this page be for, and what do we lose by choosing?
- Is breadth an asset or a liability, and how would you stage it?
- What is our one sentence?
- Where does the evidence rating belong — proof point, or organising idea?
- What do we do with traffic before the App Store listing is live?

**Phase 2 — Design (4 weeks).** Full responsive design for the home page and
one secondary page, with the reasoning attached. Mobile first; the majority of
our traffic is a phone in one hand.

**Phase 3 — Build support (2 weeks).** We build it. We want you close enough to
reject our compromises.

## 10. How we will judge the work

- A stranger can state what we do, correctly, after ten seconds. We will test
  this the same way we tested the current site.
- A reader who never watches or clicks anything still gets the whole argument.
- Reading age of 13.
- The category difference is understood in the first viewport.
- It works, and looks intentional, at 360px.
- Nothing on it is untrue.

## 11. Practicalities

- **Timeline:** we would like Phase 1 to start within two weeks. App Store
  approval is imminent and we would rather launch onto a good page late than a
  poor page on time.
- **Budget:** to be discussed on scope, but assume a serious commitment rather
  than a landing-page refresh.
- **Decision-maker:** one founder. Fast decisions, strong opinions, and he will
  tell you when something is not good enough. You will get direct access.
- **What we most want from you:** the thing we cannot do ourselves. We can
  write, we can build, and we can be honest about our own failures. What we
  have not managed is to make someone who has never heard of us *want* this in
  the first ten seconds.

# Response — the five Phase 1 questions, answered and built

Written 5 September 2026, from the agency chair, and implemented the same day.

## 1. Who should this page be for, and what do we lose by choosing?

**People who already know what to do and cannot fit it into a week.**

That is the one description all five reviewers fit. Sam knows she should
sleep and move; she has no time. Jo knows what drinking costs him; he cannot
change the evening. Priya is not short of advice; she is short of hours. Even
Dev, the most product-literate, is really saying "I run four apps to do this".

We lose two people, deliberately. The beginner who wants to be told one thing
and held by the hand — this will feel like a lot. And the specialist who wants
the deepest training app on the store — we are not it, and pretending
otherwise is what put a barbell in our shop window.

## 2. Is breadth an asset or a liability, and how do you stage it?

An asset that must be **demonstrated before it is claimed**. Listing nine
things reads as thin. Showing one real day that happens to contain training,
food, light, focused work and a protected lunch reads as capable, and the
reader counts the breadth themselves.

So the hero is now a single planned Monday. The list of coaches comes after
the demonstration, and its heading answers the objection rather than
restating the count: they talk to each other. The training knows how you
slept; the work blocks know when you train; lunch is protected from both.
That is the sentence breadth earns.

## 3. What is our one sentence?

> You already know what you should be doing. The hard part is fitting it into
> a real week.

The product sentence follows from it, not the other way round. Every previous
attempt opened with what we are — an operating system, seven coaches, a
library — which asks the reader to care about us before we have shown we
understand them.

## 4. Where does the evidence rating belong?

**The organising idea.** It is the only thing in this market that nobody else
does, it is expensive to fake, and four of five cold readers named it
unprompted as where trust began. It has been promoted into the first
viewport as a promise — everything it asks of you shows how strong the
evidence is — with the costly half attached: 202 of 317.

## 5. What do we do with traffic before the listing is live?

Stop making a badge the main event. A store button that cannot be pressed is
the one thing every visitor tries, and both our impatient reviewers hit it and
asked why they were there.

The free thing is now the action — **See your first week, free** — and the
store is a status line beside it. It needs no backend, contradicts no privacy
claim, and turns the gap before approval into use rather than a dead end. On
approval day the badge becomes the primary and one constant changes.

## What this pass also corrected

Writing the coaches section caught a mistake of our own making. We had begun
saying "nine areas", which came from counting pillars in the practice library
rather than coaches in the app. The app has seven coaches. Sleep and mind are
not among them — they are practices scheduled across all seven, which is both
the accurate description and the only place meditation, breathing and a
wind-down were ever going to be named. Corrected before it shipped.

The plain-language guard then failed the build because the word "coach"
appeared without ever being defined — the exact confusion the user testing
found. It now says, before the word is leaned on: each coach is one part of
your life that the app plans for, not a person and not a chatbot.
