# Five readers, none of whom had heard of us

Five reviewers read the home page cold on 4 Sep 2026 — no context, no source,
no brief beyond who they were. Sam (41, two kids, does not train), Dev (29,
lifts four times a week, wears a Whoop), Jo (47, drinking most nights and wants
to stop), Priya (44, executive, ninety seconds), and a plain-language reviewer
assessing it as public health information.

Four of the five would not have installed it. The fifth could not, because it
is not in the store.

## What all five said

**1. The barbell disqualifies almost everyone.** `Bench 4 × 6 at 90kg` is the
first concrete example on the page. Sam: "confirmed: this is not for me."
Jo: "it looks like it's for a man who lifts." Priya: "I nearly closed it here."
Only Dev wanted it — and wanted it higher. One page cannot hold both.

**2. 5,376 is worse than useless.** Sam read it as "a developer's changelog".
Dev: "you counted a cartesian product; my spreadsheet also generates infinite
unique plans. Hashing proves nothing about quality." Priya: "5,376 is a worse
number than 1; it means nothing was chosen for me." Delete it.

**3. The disclaimers cost us the trust they were meant to protect.** Three
separate passages arguing that our screenshots are real. Sam: "nobody says that
unless someone accused them. It made me wonder what's fake." Jo: "a company
arguing with a critic, not talking to me." Priya: "three defences against an
accusation nobody made." This is the clearest case in the review of a fix that
made things worse: they were written to be scrupulous and they read as guilty.

**4. The price is hidden at 88% of the way down.** Priya: "you are cheap and
generous and you hid both." AU$89.99 a year for all seven areas, nothing
withheld, hardest-moment support free forever — the strongest trust asset on
the page, behind 250 lines of epistemology.

**5. Nobody can install it.** "Coming to the App Store · in review" with no
alternative action. Sam: "then why am I here?" Dev: "everything above is
unfalsifiable until it ships."

**6. The one thing that works is the admission.** Four of five named it
unprompted. Jo: "admitting your own library is mostly not-strong is costly and
therefore believable." Dev: "a costed claim." Keep it, and build on it.

## The findings that are not opinions

Two are correctness problems, not design ones.

**The privacy contradiction.** "Nothing leaves your phone" sits near
"connecting this web profile to the app is the next product connection". Jo,
who would be typing about her drinking, read that as: it is on your servers.
It is not — the preview is in the browser and there is no database, which
`tests/no-database.test.mjs` enforces. But the page never says so, and the
person most exposed by the ambiguity is the person least able to afford it.

**The alcohol content needs a floor.** "Most of it comes back if the last drink
lands three or more hours before bed" is a specific promise about a stranger's
body, and it quietly reframes stopping as timing. Jo: "I said I want to stop
drinking. This teaches me to drink earlier." Nothing beside that content
acknowledges dependence; the only signpost is in the footer.

## The strategic problem

The page describes a product for seven areas of life and proves it with a
barbell. That single choice sorts every reader: it recruits Dev and rejects
Sam, Jo and Priya. And Dev — the one it recruits — says breadth is the reason
he would not buy: "seven domains from one team means none of them are deep.
This is the single biggest credibility leak on the page."

So the page is currently optimised for the one reader who is least likely to
convert, at the cost of the three who might.

The material to fix it is already written and buried. Priya called the "bring a
goal we have never seen" trio — write the book, save a house deposit, be a
calmer parent — "your best section, and it's at line 120". That is the only
place on the page where three of the five readers saw their own life.

## What the reviewers agreed to delete

The 5,376 block. All three anti-camera passages. "The system reflects it back
before revealing your architecture." "Same engine underneath: goal → ladder →
program → check-in → adaptation." The academic bibliography. Three of the four
restatements of the evidence-rating point. Every gym number above the fold.
