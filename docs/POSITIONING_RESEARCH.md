# The research corpus as a selling point

Positioning input for the website session. **Copy and argument only — no
deployment.** Every number below is verified; the "do not claim yet"
section at the end is binding.

## The proposition, in Isaac's words

> People purchase and listen to self-improvement to get better, but they
> can't possibly remember or plan out these improvements into their daily
> lives as it's too varied and complicated — our app and coaches will do
> this for them.

That is the strongest sentence anyone has written about this product, and
the research run is what makes it defensible rather than aspirational.

## The gap it names

Somebody who follows this category properly has consumed hundreds of hours
of Huberman, Attia, Ferriss, Rhonda Patrick. They know morning light
matters. They know about Zone 2, protein, sleep debt, deliberate practice.

And their Tuesday is unchanged.

Not through weakness. Because **advice arrives as a pile and a life needs a
plan.** Nobody holds three hundred practices in their head, works out which
four apply to them this month, sequences them, and finds the hour. The
podcast cannot do it — it does not know your roster, your kids, or that
you already walk the dog.

Every competitor sells more content into that gap. We are the only one
selling the thing that closes it.

## Three claims, each verifiable

**1. We read the field, then checked it.**

- 836 episodes routed to a pillar from a corpus of 36,198 transcripts
- roughly 180-200 episodes actually mined
- **over 500 papers opened** at publisher, PMC or registry level
- every load-bearing paper checked for retraction

**2. We grade it honestly, including when that costs us.**

Every practice carries A-E, in plain words, with the reasoning
checkable. Across 113 new candidates the run produced **two A grades.**

The proof is what it did under pressure: with a launch to sauna owners
waiting on it, the round graded sauna **C**, sauna-then-cold **E**, and
infrared **D** — because that is what the papers support. A round that
came back with sauna at A would have been worth nothing.

**3. We will tell you what to stop.**

The run found a paper retracted for fabricated data sitting underneath one
of our own shipped cards, and we are fixing it rather than quietly leaving
it. It found four well-known communicators all citing one study for a
protein number that study's own analysis does not support. It found a
widely repeated relationship statistic that appears in no paper at all.

Nobody else in this category publishes that, because for most of them the
claims they would have to retract are the ones they are selling.

## The finding that is the positioning

The run measured something nobody in this market says out loud:

> The four best-evidenced researchers in workplace recovery return **zero
> podcast episodes between them.** So do eleven of the best-evidenced
> names in learning and mental health. Meanwhile "burnout" returns twenty
> episodes by no burnout researcher.

**The more rigorous the science, the less likely a podcast covers it.**

Podcasts find what is being sold and argued about. The literature finds
what is true and quiet. An app built on either alone is wrong in a
different direction — and every competitor is built on the first one.

That is the sharpest line we have and it is measured, not asserted.

## Copy directions

**Headline territory** — the gap, not the library:

- "You already know what to do. You have listened to hundreds of hours of
  it. This is the part that puts it in Tuesday."
- "The reading is done. Now it has to fit around a real week."
- "Seven coaches that have read the research so you do not have to
  remember it."

**Where the grading belongs** — as honesty, never as a boast:

- "Every practice is graded A to E. Most of ours are not an A. Little of
  what anyone can teach you about your own life is. You get to see which
  is which."

That line already exists in the app and it is the best thing we have
written. Lead with it.

**The time-back angle** — the most quotable and least copyable:

- "Most apps only add things. We will also tell you what to stop."
- Foam rolling before lifting. The ten-thousand-hours story. Cold plunges
  after a lifting session, if muscle is the goal. Each with its citation.

**What to avoid:**

- Do not lead with a protocol count. It reads as content volume, which is
  the competitor's game and the thing that overwhelms people.
- Do not imply endorsement by any named communicator. Attribution is
  credit, never endorsement, and the site must say so where names appear.
- No white coats, no brain imagery, no "backed by science" as a badge.
  The grade *is* the claim; a badge is what people use when they have no
  grade.

## Why it sells

**It reframes the purchase.** Not another subscription competing for
attention against the podcasts — the thing that makes the podcasts you
already paid attention to actually pay off. That is a different, easier
sale, and it makes the audience's existing consumption an asset rather
than a competitor.

**It is proof, not adjectives.** Everyone says evidence-based. We can show
a grade distribution where most of it is not an A, and a public list of
things we removed. Verifiable modesty is far more persuasive than
unverifiable confidence, and it is exactly what a sceptical, well-read
audience responds to.

**It matches the buyer.** The sauna owners, the ring wearers, the people in
the association and employer channels are the ones who have already done
the reading. They are the hardest audience to impress with content and the
easiest to win with rigour.

**And it compounds.** Every round adds cards, regrades, and another entry
in the register. A competitor starting today is not a year behind on
content — they are a year behind on the checking, and the checking is the
part that cannot be hurried.

## Do not claim yet

Binding, and the website session must hold these.

- **The 113 candidates are not merged.** The library is **204**, not 317.
  No count above 204 until they ship.
- **The app is not on the App Store.** Version 1.0 is Waiting for Review.
  Nothing may imply availability.
- **No implied endorsement** by any communicator, ever.
- **No health outcome claims.** Education, never advice, and never
  "prescription".
- The retracted-paper find is a **strength worth telling**, but only
  alongside the fix. "We found it and corrected it" is the story. "We
  shipped it" on its own is not.
